Chapter 3. Image Grain

Rauf pulled the monitor toward the edge of the desk. On the screen was a corridor filmed from above at an angle: the near wall took up a good third of the image, and the far turn dissolved into gray noise. Asgar recognized the light he had been standing under and leaned forward so sharply that the chair legs scraped across the floor.

The booth was by the wall.

In the recording, its red looked muddy brown. The glass panels blended together, the top ran into the glare from the light, but it was standing there—whole, from roof to base. Asgar stared at it and felt his teeth gradually unclench. The whole time the doctor had been asking questions, the whole time Rauf had been writing down the answers, he had kept his jaw tight.

"There," he said. "There it is."

Katya was sitting beside him. She leaned closer to the screen without touching it.

"Is that the one you went into?"

Asgar nodded. He even smiled, briefly and involuntarily. He turned to Rauf, expecting the same change from him.

The investigator put his hand on the mouse.

"Watch the whole clip first. Then we'll stop it."

"You saw it before? While you were looking for me?"

"Yes."

The smile disappeared.

"And you were asking whether I'd imagined it?"

"I was asking what you remembered. I got your description before I showed you the recording."

"You could have told me right away that it existed."

"I could have. Then I wouldn't know which details you remembered on your own."

Asgar looked away. He wanted to argue, but Rauf started the video, and from the left edge of the screen Asgar himself walked into view.

He was walking with the phone to his ear, head slightly lowered. He stopped, shifted to the side. Asgar could see the movement of his own elbow and remembered how at that moment he had pressed the mobile closer, trying to hear Katya. There was no sound on the recording. He knew the stands had been roaring above the corridor, that he had had to raise his voice, but the screen showed a man calmly examining a red box.

The door began to open.

Asgar moved even closer. In the recording, a dark strip appeared between the door and the wall; a shoulder came through it, then a leg. The man climbed out into the corridor. His hand lingered on the door, then let go. He walked past Asgar toward the far turn.

Katya gripped the edge of the seat with her fingers.

"Is that him?"

"Yes."

Asgar waited for the stop before the turn. The man reached the edge of the lit area and turned his head. Because of the camera angle, his face remained a pale blot above a dark shoulder.

"Stop it here."

Rauf paused the recording.

Asgar stood up and bent toward the monitor. His eyes searched the gray patches that should have formed a face. He remembered the thinness, the beard, the gray at the temple, the slightly open mouth. He wanted to match memory to the still blot on the screen, to force the image into a recognizable person.

"Can you enlarge it?"

Rauf zoomed in on the fragment. The head filled half the window. Large squares appeared along its edge; one dark patch crossed the cheek.

"What about sharpening it?"

The investigator opened another file. The same frame, cropped and with more contrast. The face looked clearer in it: a long nose emerged, a dark hollow beneath the cheekbone. Asgar lingered on it, then looked back at the first frame.

"Go back."

Rauf brought up the original image.

"The processed one again."

He switched.

Asgar straightened.

"The nose looks different here."

"That's why we don't use this version for identification."

"Who processed it?"

"The specialist checking the recordings. This is an intermediate result."

Asgar sat down again. A minute earlier, it had seemed to him that once they enlarged the face, the hardest part would be over. The man would have a name. They could find where he lived, ask where the booth had come from. The person on the screen remained a collection of blotches, and the clearer blotches were already beginning to suggest a face he did not remember.

"Show it in motion. No zoom."

Rauf returned to the recording.

The man glanced back and disappeared around the turn. Asgar on the screen watched him for a few more seconds, then turned toward the booth.

"He was looking at it," Asgar said. "When he stopped."

"Why do you think that?"

"I was standing beside it. He was looking past me, at the door. I turned around myself to check."

Rauf made a note.

On the screen, Asgar approached the booth, opened the door, and squeezed inside. Katya raised a hand to her lips. The red door closed. A dark movement remained in one of the panels and soon could no longer be made out.

Asgar waited for the jolt. He remembered his knees buckling, his shoulder hitting the panel. In the recording, no one fell, the camera did not move. The booth blurred along the edges several times. Then there was empty space by the wall.

Rauf stopped the video.

The fan rattled in the room.

Asgar reached for the mouse, but the investigator caught it first.

"What do you want to see?"

"Before it disappears. Frame by frame."

Rauf moved the recording back.

The booth appeared again. One more step forward—the red edge broke into bands. The next frame was gray.

"Is there a dropout here?"

"The frame is corrupted," Rauf said. "We don't know why yet."

"And the rest of the image?"

Asgar pointed to the corner of the screen. There, by the service entrance, the edge of the open door was visible. Rauf went back a few frames and ran the sequence again. The door shifted slightly, stopped; the light over the corridor kept flickering.

"So the whole frame didn't disappear," Asgar said.

"Right. And the recording from the camera in the concourse continues at the same time."

"Show me."

People were moving in the other image. A man adjusted something in his pocket; two others stood by the wall with a phone. Asgar recognized them: he had passed them on his way into the corridor. Now he could see one of them tapping the screen with a finger and turning it toward the other.

No one ran. No one even looked toward the service door.

Asgar watched them longer than necessary. He wanted to stop one of them, force him to raise his head. A few steps and the man would have been beside the booth before it disappeared. But the red patch had already vanished from the neighboring corridor, and the people kept looking at their phone.

"Does this record sound?" he asked.

"No."

He took his hands off the desk.

Katya turned to Rauf.

"When you told me he doesn't come out on the recording... Is this what you meant?"

"This, and checking the other exits."

"You didn't show me how it disappears."

"That's why I didn't. I didn't understand what it was myself."

Katya looked at the monitor. Then she moved her chair closer to Asgar's, leaving no gap between them.

Rauf opened the recording dated August twentieth. Asgar recognized the dim light of the service corridor, his own figure beside the red door. He came out, stepped back. The booth distorted; Asgar on the screen recoiled toward the opposite wall. After it disappeared, he approached the concrete, touched it, and jerked his hands away.

Katya quietly said his name.

He did not answer. From the outside, his movements looked abrupt and helpless. There he was pressing his palms against the wall, jumping away from it, running toward the exit. Asgar remembered every movement. After the sound of the stadium had cut off, only minutes had passed before he got out: he had knocked on the glass, called for people, tried Katya twice, then answered the old telephone and spoken to an unknown woman.

"Again," Katya said.

Rauf moved the recording back. She watched Asgar on the screen step away from the wall and the red spread into bands.

"Enough." Katya moved away from the monitor and sat upright. "They wouldn't let me into this corridor. On Sunday. I stood by the service door and asked someone to go in and look one more time. They told me it was empty and there was nothing to look at."

She shifted her gaze from the screen to Rauf.

"It was empty, I'm not arguing. I want you to write down that I asked."

Rauf pulled his notebook closer.

"I will."

"Did you check the clocks on these cameras?"

"We did. The date of the match and today's exit are both confirmed by other recordings and witnesses."

"And the files? Is this an export? I need the original segment, before the booth appears and after. Both days."

Rauf closed the playback window.

"You won't be given copies right now."

Asgar stared at the empty desktop.

"Why?"

"The review is still ongoing. You can tell us what needs to be checked and ask questions. The materials stay with us."

"That's me in the recording."

"Yes."

"Then give it to me. I know people who can examine the file. Not invent a face—look at what happened to the image."

Rauf turned fully toward him.

"No. I'm not giving you a copy right now. Until we finish the review, it stays here. You send it to friends, and an hour later I'll have ten versions and not a single uncontaminated witness."

"I'm not going to post it online."

"And I'm not handing over material based on a promise."

Asgar pushed his chair back. In the cramped room, he nearly hit Katya with his knee and stopped. She took her hand off his elbow, letting him stand.

"You've been looking at this for four days," he said. "What did you find out?"

Rauf held his gaze.

"You didn't leave through any of the exits we checked. The booth is on the recording. The man too. Today you came back and told us about things the camera doesn't show."

"So I didn't make it up."

"The booth and the man—no."

Asgar waited for more, but Rauf added nothing.

He sat down again. The relief he had been waiting for did not come. The doctor could still find a reason for a memory blackout. He could be frightened by his own condition, agree to tests, go to a hospital. But the camera had seen the red booth. While Asgar had been talking about it in the security room, it had already existed in a file on someone else's computer.

"Where did the man go?" he asked.

Rauf opened the recording again and brought up the frame before the turn.

"Around that corner."

"I saw that. Where after that?"

"Come on."

***

Beyond the turn, the corridor ended after only a few steps.

Asgar had expected more lights, a door outside, stairs. Instead his eyes hit the end wall, and he walked toward it.

Rauf followed. Katya stayed by the turn; from here Asgar could see her shoulder and part of her face. She was looking toward him without coming farther in.

"Twelve meters," the investigator said. "From the corner to the end."

Asgar touched the end wall. Ran a hand along the joint, bent toward the floor. The concrete was uneven, spotted with paint in places. He raised his head, examined the ceiling, then slowly came back along the other wall.

"Was anything here closed off? Blocked?"

"Not when we inspected it."

"A door could have been concealed."

"We checked the walls and the floor plan."

Asgar looked at the end wall again. The man had gone exactly this way. Stepped around the corner confidently, without stopping before it. At the time, Asgar had thought he simply knew where he was going.

"And back?"

"On the recordings we reviewed, he doesn't come back."

"How long did you watch after he went in?"

"The entire evening and night. Then spot checks after that. We never saw that man again."

Asgar turned toward him.

"There's only one way out. If he left, he had to pass the camera."

"That's exactly why I haven't closed the case."

He spoke calmly, and Asgar wanted to grab him by the shoulder and turn him toward the wall: there it is, the end of the corridor, there is nowhere to go. But Rauf could already see the wall. He was standing beside him, had already measured the distance and written it down.

Asgar walked to the end again. Stopped with an arm's length between himself and the concrete. There was enough space behind him to turn around, but he had to make himself do it. He did not want to lose sight of the wall for even a second.

Katya called:

"Asgar. What are you looking for?"

"I don't know."

He walked back to her. At the corner itself, he stopped and looked back. The empty stretch of corridor was completely visible from end to end.

"He would have come back past me," Asgar said. "While I was standing by the booth. I would have seen him."

"While you were standing by the booth—yes. After that, you weren't here either."

Asgar wanted to snap at him but held back. Rauf had left another qualification. Asgar needed at least one place where he could put a period and stop doubting.

Katya took his hand.

***

Back in the security room, Rauf let him watch the man in motion one more time.

Asgar did not ask to enlarge the face now. He watched the man come out, straighten, walk past. In that short section of footage, the man sometimes sped up, sometimes placed one foot more carefully. Asgar tried to remember whether he had heard him say anything. Only footsteps. Katya on the mobile. His own voice.

"Ever seen him before?" Rauf asked.

"I don't remember anyone like him."

"Did he give you anything? Touch you?"

"No."

"Try to speak to you?"

Asgar shook his head.

On the screen, the man looked back at the door.

"And here," Rauf said, "you decided to look at the booth after him?"

"Yes. I thought he'd left something there."

Rauf wrote down the answer.

Asgar rubbed a hand over his face. He wanted to add something useful. Height, age, a distinguishing mark. He had been close—closer than the investigator was sitting now. He could have seen. But he had been trying to hear Katya, letting the man pass, looking for quiet. The face had not remained whole in memory, and every replay risked replacing what little still remained.

"Enough for now," he said.

Rauf stopped the video.

Katya stood and reached for her bag.

"Can we go?"

The investigator asked them to keep their phones on and confirmed where Asgar would be that evening. Asgar gave the home address. Rauf wrote it beside the number and closed the notebook.

At the door, Asgar turned back.

"If you find this man, call me immediately."

"I will."

"Even if he says he doesn't know anything."

Rauf held his gaze.

"You think he knows?"

Asgar looked at the frozen frame. The man stood by the turn, head turned toward the red door. Asgar found nothing else in the image.

"I want to ask him."

They went out into the concourse. Katya walked beside him, holding his arm. The daylight by the exit was bright; engines and short car horns came from outside. Asgar took out his mobile to silence it and left it as it was.

Near the outer door, he stopped.

"You saw it?"

Katya turned toward him.

"The booth. In the recording. You saw it?"

She tightened her grip on his arm.

"I saw it."

Asgar nodded and stepped outside with her.
