Chapter 3. Image Grain

Rauf pulled the monitor closer to the edge of the desk. On-screen, the corridor had been captured from above at an angle: the near wall swallowed almost a third of the frame, and the far turn dissolved into gray noise. Asgar recognized the lamp beneath which he had been standing and leaned forward so sharply that the chair legs scraped the floor.

The booth was there against the wall.

On the recording, its red looked dirty brown. The glass panes blurred together, the roof ran into the glare from a lamp, but it stood there in full—from top to base. Asgar stared at it and felt his teeth slowly unclench. All through the doctor’s questions, all through Rauf writing down his answers, he had kept his jaw locked.

“There,” he said. “That’s it.”

Katya sat beside him. She leaned closer to the screen without touching it.

“That’s the one you went into?”

Asgar nodded. He even smiled, briefly and involuntarily. He turned to Rauf, expecting to see the same change in him.

The investigator rested a hand on the mouse.

“Watch the whole segment first. Then we’ll stop it.”

“You’d already seen the booth? While you were looking for me?”

“Yes.”

The smile vanished.

“And you still asked whether I might have imagined it?”

“I asked what you remembered. I wanted your description before I showed you the recording.”

“You could have told me straight away that it was real.”

“I could have. Then I wouldn’t know which details you remembered on your own.”

Asgar looked away. He wanted to argue, but Rauf started the video, and Asgar himself entered from the left edge of the frame.

He was walking with the phone to his ear, head slightly lowered. He stopped, shifted sideways. Asgar watched his own elbow move and remembered pressing the mobile tighter at that moment, trying to hear Katya. The recording had no sound. He knew the stands had been roaring above the corridor, knew he had been raising his voice, but the screen showed a man calmly studying a red box.

The door began to open.

Asgar edged closer. On-screen, a dark strip appeared between the panel and the wall; a shoulder emerged, then a leg. The man squeezed into the corridor. One hand lingered on the door, released it. He walked past Asgar toward the far bend.

Katya gripped the edge of her seat.

“Is that him?”

“Yes.”

Asgar waited for the man to stop before the turn. He reached the edge of the lit area and turned his head. From the camera angle, his face remained a pale blot above a dark shoulder.

“Stop there.”

Rauf paused the video.

Asgar stood and bent toward the monitor. His eyes caught on gray patches that ought to have assembled into a face. He remembered the gauntness, the beard, gray hair at the temple, the slightly open mouth. He wanted to force memory and the still image together until they became a person he could recognize.

“Can you enlarge it?”

Rauf zoomed in. The head filled half the window. Large blocks appeared along its edge; one dark patch cut across the cheek.

“Can you sharpen it?”

The investigator opened another file. The same frame, cropped and with stronger contrast. The face looked clearer there: a long nose seemed to emerge, a dark hollow beneath the cheekbone. Asgar stared at it, then shifted his eyes back to the original.

“Go back.”

Rauf restored the source image.

“Now the processed one again.”

He switched.

Asgar straightened.

“The nose looks different here.”

“That’s why we don’t use this version for identification.”

“Who processed it?”

“A specialist reviewing the footage. It’s an intermediate result.”

Asgar sat again. A minute earlier it had seemed obvious: enlarge the face and the hardest part would be over. The man would have a name. They could find where he lived, ask where the booth had come from. The person on the screen remained a cluster of stains, and the clearer stains were already beginning to offer him a face he did not remember.

“Show him moving. No zoom.”

Rauf returned to the video.

The man looked back and disappeared around the bend. On-screen, Asgar watched after him for several seconds, then turned toward the booth.

“He was looking at it,” Asgar said. “When he stopped.”

“Why do you think that?”

“I was standing beside it. He was looking past me, at the door. I turned around to check.”

Rauf made a note.

On the screen, Asgar approached the booth, opened the door, squeezed inside. Katya raised one hand to her mouth. The red panel closed. For a while, dark movement remained visible in one of the glass sections, then became indistinguishable.

Asgar waited for the jolt. He remembered his knees buckling, his shoulder striking the wall. On the recording, no one fell; the camera did not move. The booth blurred at the edges several times.

Then there was empty space beside the wall.

Rauf stopped the video.

The fan rattled in the room.

Asgar reached for the mouse, but the investigator took it first.

“What do you want to see?”

“Just before it disappears. Frame by frame.”

Rauf scrubbed backward.

The booth returned. One frame forward—the red edge broke into strips. The next frame was gray.

“Dropped frame?”

“The frame is corrupted,” Rauf said. “We don’t know why yet.”

“What about the rest of the picture?”

Asgar pointed to the corner of the screen. Near the service entrance, the edge of the open door was visible. Rauf backed up several frames and played the sequence again. The door shifted a little, stopped; the lamp above the corridor kept flickering.

“So the whole frame didn’t drop,” Asgar said.

“No. And the concourse camera continues recording through the same moment.”

“Show me.”

On the other image, people were walking. A man adjusted something in his pocket; two others stood against the wall over a phone. Asgar recognized them—he had passed them on the way into the corridor. Now he could see one tapping the screen and turning it toward the other.

No one ran.

No one even looked toward the service door.

Asgar watched them longer than necessary. He wanted to stop one of them, force him to lift his head. A few steps and the man would have been beside the booth before it disappeared. But the red patch was already gone from the adjoining corridor, and the two men kept staring at their phone.

“Does this camera record sound?” he asked.

“No.”

Asgar took his hands off the desk.

Katya turned to Rauf.

“When you told me he didn’t come out on the footage… Is this what you meant?”

“This, and checking the other exits.”

“You didn’t show me the booth disappearing.”

“That’s why I didn’t. I didn’t understand what I was looking at either.”

Katya looked at the monitor. Then she pulled her chair closer to Asgar until there was no gap left between them.

Rauf opened the recording dated August twentieth. Asgar recognized the dim technical corridor, his own figure beside the red door. He came out and stepped back. The booth distorted; the Asgar on-screen lurched toward the opposite wall. Once it vanished, he walked up to the concrete, touched it, snatched his hands away.

Katya said his name quietly.

He did not answer. Seen from outside, his movements looked abrupt and helpless. There he was slapping his palms against the wall, recoiling from it, running for the exit. Asgar remembered every movement. Only minutes had passed for him between the stadium noise cutting off and reaching the exit: he had knocked on the glass, shouted for help, called Katya twice, then answered the old telephone and spoken to an unknown woman.

“Again,” Katya said.

Rauf wound the recording back. She watched Asgar on-screen retreat from the wall, watched the red spread into bands and disappear.

“That’s enough.” Katya leaned away from the monitor and sat upright. “They wouldn’t let me into that corridor. On Sunday. I stood at the service door and asked them to go in and check again. They told me it was empty and there was nothing to look at.”

She moved her gaze from the screen to Rauf.

“It was empty. I’m not arguing with that. I want it written down that I asked.”

Rauf pulled his notebook closer.

“I’ll write it down.”

“Did you verify the timestamps on these cameras?”

“Yes. The date of the match and today’s reappearance are both confirmed by other recordings and by witnesses.”

“And the files? Is this an export? I want the original segment, from before the booth appears to after it’s gone. Both days.”

Rauf closed the playback window.

“You won’t be given copies right now.”

Asgar stared at the empty desktop.

“Why?”

“The review is still ongoing. You can tell us what you want examined and ask questions. The material stays with us.”

“That’s me in the recording.”

“Yes.”

“Then give it to me. I know people who can inspect the file. Not invent a face—figure out what actually happened to the image.”

Rauf turned fully toward him.

“No. I’m not giving you a copy right now. Until we finish our review, it stays here. You send it to friends, and an hour later I get ten interpretations and not one uncontaminated witness.”

“I’m not going to put it on the internet.”

“And I’m not handing over evidence on the strength of a promise.”

Asgar pushed his chair back. In the cramped room he nearly struck Katya’s knee with his own and stopped. She took her hand from his elbow, letting him stand.

“You’ve had four days to look at this,” he said. “What have you found?”

Rauf held his gaze.

“You did not leave through any of the exits we checked. The booth is in the footage. So is the man. Today you returned and described things the camera does not show.”

“So I didn’t make it up.”

“The booth and the man? No.”

Asgar waited for more, but Rauf added nothing.

He sat again. The relief he had been waiting for did not come. A doctor might still find some explanation for the missing memory. He could be afraid of his own condition, agree to tests, check into a hospital. But the camera had seen the red booth. By the time Asgar was describing it in the security room, it already existed as a file on someone else’s computer.

“Where did the man go?” he asked.

Rauf reopened the recording and brought up the frame just before the bend.

“Around that corner.”

“I saw that. Where after that?”

“Come with me.”

***

Beyond the turn, the corridor ended after a few steps.

Asgar had expected more lights, a door outside, stairs. Instead his gaze struck the end wall. He walked toward it.

Rauf followed. Katya remained at the bend; from here Asgar could see one shoulder and part of her face. She watched him without coming farther in.

“Twelve meters,” the investigator said. “From the corner to the end.”

Asgar touched the wall. Ran his hand along the seam, bent toward the floor. The concrete was uneven, blotched with paint in places. He raised his head, checked the ceiling, then slowly came back along the opposite wall.

“Was anything here blocked off? Covered?”

“Not when we inspected it.”

“There could have been a concealed door.”

“We checked the walls and the building plans.”

Asgar looked at the end wall again. The man had gone this way. He had stepped around the corner confidently and had not stopped in front of it. At the time Asgar had assumed he simply knew where he was going.

“And back?”

“There’s no return on the footage we reviewed.”

“How long did you watch after he went in?”

“The entire evening and night. Then spot checks after that. We never saw that man again.”

Asgar turned to him.

“There’s only one way in here. If he came out, he had to pass the camera.”

“That’s why I haven’t closed the case.”

He said it calmly, and Asgar wanted to grab him by the shoulder and turn him toward the wall: there it is, the corridor ends, there is nowhere to go. But Rauf could see the wall. He was standing beside it. He had already measured the distance and written it down.

Asgar walked to the end again. He stopped an arm’s length from the concrete. There was enough space behind him to turn, but he had to make himself do it. He did not want to lose sight of the wall for even a second.

Katya called to him.

“Asgar. What are you looking for?”

“I don’t know.”

He went back toward her. At the corner he stopped and looked behind him. The empty stretch of corridor was visible in its entirety.

“He would have come back past me,” Asgar said. “While I was standing by the booth. I would have seen him.”

“While you were standing by the booth, yes. After that, you were gone too.”

Asgar almost snapped at him, then held back. Rauf had left himself another qualification. Asgar needed at least one place where the sentence could end and doubt could stop.

Katya took his hand.

***

Back in the security room, Rauf let them watch the man in motion one more time.

Asgar no longer asked to enlarge the face. He watched the man come out, straighten, pass by. Over the short stretch of footage he would quicken, then set one foot down more carefully. Asgar tried to remember whether the man had said anything. Only footsteps. Katya in his mobile. His own voice.

“Have you ever seen him before?” Rauf asked.

“I don’t remember anyone like him.”

“Did he give you anything? Touch you?”

“No.”

“Try to speak to you?”

Asgar shook his head.

On-screen, the man looked back at the door.

“And here,” Rauf said, “you decided to look at the booth because he did?”

“Yes. I thought he’d left something there.”

Rauf wrote down the answer.

Asgar rubbed a palm over his face. He wanted to add something useful. Height, age, a distinguishing mark. The man had been right beside him—closer than the investigator was sitting now. He could have looked. But he had been trying to hear Katya, making room for a stranger, searching for quiet. The face had not survived whole in his memory, and every replay risked replacing what little remained.

“That’s enough for now,” he said.

Rauf stopped the video.

Katya stood and reached for her bag.

“Can we go?”

The investigator asked them to keep their phones on and confirmed where Asgar would be that evening. Asgar gave his home address. Rauf wrote it beside the number and closed the notebook.

At the door, Asgar turned back.

“If you find that man, call me immediately.”

“I will.”

“Even if he says he knows nothing.”

Rauf held his gaze for a moment.

“You think he knows?”

Asgar looked at the paused frame. The man stood by the bend with his head turned toward the red door. There was nothing else in the image Asgar could find.

“I want to ask him.”

They went out into the concourse. Katya walked beside him, holding his arm. The daylight near the exit was bright; from outside came engines and short bursts of car horns. Asgar took out his mobile to silence it, then left the sound as it was.

Near the outer door, he stopped.

“You saw it?”

Katya turned to him.

“The booth. In the recording. You saw it?”

She tightened her grip on his hand.

“I saw it.”

Asgar nodded and stepped outside with her.
