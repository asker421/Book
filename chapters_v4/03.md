Chapter 3. Image Grain

Rauf pulled the monitor to the edge of the desk. On the screen was a corridor filmed from above at an angle: the near wall took up a good third of the image, and the far turn dissolved into gray noise. Asgar recognized the light he had been standing under and leaned forward so sharply that the chair legs scraped across the floor.

The booth was against the wall.

On the recording, its red color looked dirty brown. The glass panels blurred together, the top ran into a glare from the light, but it was there—whole, from roof to base. Asgar looked at it and felt his teeth slowly unclench. The entire time the doctor had been asking questions, the entire time Rauf had been writing down answers, he had kept his jaw clenched.

"There," he said. "There it is."

Katya was sitting beside him. She leaned closer to the screen without touching it.

"Is that the one you went into?"

Asgar nodded. He even smiled, briefly and involuntarily. He turned to Rauf, expecting the same change in him.

The investigator put a hand on the mouse.

"Watch the whole segment first. Then we'll stop it."

"You saw it before? While you were looking for me?"

"Yes."

The smile disappeared.

"And you still asked whether I might have imagined it?"

"I asked what you remembered. I got your description before I showed you the recording."

"You could have told me right away that it existed."

"I could have. Then I wouldn't know which details you remembered on your own."

Asgar looked away. He wanted to argue, but Rauf started the video, and Asgar himself entered from the left side of the screen.

He was walking with the phone to his ear, head slightly lowered. He stopped and stepped sideways. Asgar saw the movement of his own elbow and remembered pressing the mobile closer at that moment, trying to hear Katya. There was no sound on the recording. He knew the stands had been roaring above the corridor, that he had had to raise his voice, but the screen showed a man calmly studying a red box.

The door began to open.

Asgar moved even closer. On the recording, a dark strip appeared between the door and the wall; a shoulder came through it, then a leg. The man emerged into the corridor. His hand lingered by the door, then released it. He walked past Asgar toward the far turn.

Katya gripped the edge of her seat.

"Is that him?"

"Yes."

Asgar waited for him to stop before the turn. The man reached the edge of the lit area and turned his head. Because of the angle, his face remained a pale patch above a dark shoulder.

"Stop it here."

Rauf paused the recording.

Asgar stood and bent toward the monitor. His eyes kept catching on the gray patches that were supposed to form a face. He remembered the thinness, the beard, gray at the temple, the slightly open mouth. He wanted to line up his memory with the still patch on the screen, force the image into a recognizable person.

"Can you enlarge it?"

Rauf enlarged the fragment. The head filled half the window. Large squares appeared around its edges, and one dark patch cut across the cheek.

"What about sharpening it?"

The investigator opened another file. The same frame, cropped and with higher contrast. The face looked clearer in it: a long nose emerged, a dark hollow beneath the cheekbone. Asgar lingered on it, then shifted his eyes back to the first frame.

"Go back."

Rauf brought up the original image.

"The processed one again."

He switched.

Asgar straightened.

"The nose looks different here."

"That's why we won't use this version for identification."

"Who processed it?"

"The specialist checking the recordings. This is an intermediate result."

Asgar sat down again. A minute earlier, it had seemed to him that if they could just bring the face closer, the hardest part would be over. The man would have a name. They could find where he lived, ask where the booth had come from. The person on the screen remained a collection of blotches, and the clearer blotches were already beginning to suggest a face Asgar did not remember.

"Show it moving. No enlargement."

Rauf returned to the recording.

The man looked back and disappeared around the turn. Asgar on the screen watched after him for several more seconds, then turned toward the booth.

"He was looking at it," Asgar said. "When he stopped."

"Why do you think that?"

"I was standing beside it. He was looking past me, at the door. I turned around myself to check."

Rauf made a note.

On the screen, Asgar approached the booth, opened the door, and squeezed inside. Katya raised a hand to her lips. The red door closed. Dark movement remained visible in one of the panes and soon became indistinguishable.

Asgar waited for the jolt. He remembered his knees buckling, the impact against his shoulder. On the recording, no one fell and the camera did not move. The booth blurred at the edges several times. Then there was empty space beside the wall.

Rauf stopped the video.

The fan rattled in the room.

Asgar reached for the mouse, but the investigator caught it first.

"What do you want to see?"

"Before it disappears. Frame by frame."

Rauf moved the recording back.

The booth appeared again. One step forward—the red edge broke into strips. The next frame was gray.

"Is there a dropout here?"

"The frame is damaged," Rauf said. "We don't know why yet."

"What about the rest of the image?"

Asgar pointed at the corner of the screen. Near the service entrance, the edge of the open door was visible. Rauf went back several frames and ran the sequence again. The door shifted slightly and stopped; the light above the corridor kept flickering.

"So the whole frame didn't disappear," Asgar said.

"Right. And the recording from the camera in the concourse continues during this time."

"Show me."

People were moving in the other image. A man was adjusting something in his pocket, two others stood by the wall with a phone. Asgar recognized them: he had walked past them on his way into the corridor. Now he could see one of them tapping a finger against the screen and turning it toward the other.

No one ran. No one even turned toward the service door.

Asgar watched them longer than necessary. He wanted to stop one of them, force him to raise his head. A few steps and he would have been beside the booth before it disappeared. But the red patch had already vanished from the neighboring corridor, and the men kept looking at their phone.

"Does this record sound?" he asked.

"No."

He took his hands off the desk.

Katya turned to Rauf.

"When you told me he doesn't come out on the recording... Is this what you meant?"

"This, and checking the other exits."

"You didn't show me it disappearing."

"That's why I didn't show you. I didn't understand what it was myself."

Katya looked at the monitor. Then she moved her chair closer to Asgar's, leaving no space between them.

Rauf opened the recording dated August twentieth. Asgar recognized the dim light of the technical corridor, his own figure beside the red door. He came out and stepped back. The booth distorted; Asgar on the screen flinched toward the opposite wall. After it disappeared, he approached the concrete, touched it, and jerked his hands away.

Katya quietly said his name.

He did not answer. From the outside, his movements looked sharp and helpless. There he was pressing his palms against the wall, then jumping back from it, then running toward the exit. Asgar remembered every movement. After the noise of the stadium cut off, only minutes had passed before he got out: he had knocked on the glass, called for people, called Katya twice, then answered the ringing old telephone and spoken to an unknown woman.

"Again," Katya said.

Rauf moved the recording back. She watched Asgar on the screen step away from the wall and the red shape spread into strips.

"Enough." Katya moved back from the monitor and sat upright. "They wouldn't let me into that corridor. On Sunday. I stood by the service door and asked someone to go in and look again. They told me it was empty and there was nothing to look at."

She shifted her gaze from the screen to Rauf.

"It was empty, I'm not arguing with that. I want you to write down that I asked."

Rauf pulled his notebook toward him.

"I'll write it down."

"Did you check the clocks on these cameras?"

"We did. Both the date of the match and today's exit are confirmed by other recordings and by people."

"What about the files? Is this an export? I need the original segment, before the booth appears and after. Both days."

Rauf closed the playback window.

"They won't give you copies right now."

Asgar stared at the empty desktop.

"Why?"

"The examination is still ongoing. You can tell us what needs to be checked and ask questions. The materials stay with us."

"That's me on the recording."

"Yes."

"Then give it to me. I know people who can check the file. Not draw in the face—look at what happened to the recording."

Rauf turned fully toward him.

"No. I'm not giving you a copy right now. Until we finish checking it, it stays here. Send it to people you know, and within an hour I'll have ten versions and not a single uncontaminated witness."

"I'm not going to put it on the internet."

"And I'm not going to hand over evidence based on a promise."

Asgar pushed his chair back. In the cramped room he nearly hit Katya's knee with his own and stopped. She took her hand off his elbow, letting him stand.

"You've been looking at this for four days," he said. "What did you find out?"

Rauf held his gaze.

"You did not leave through any of the exits we checked. The booth is on the recording. The man is too. Today you came back and described something the camera doesn't show."

"So I didn't make it up."

"The booth and the man—no."

Asgar waited for more, but Rauf added nothing.

He sat down again. The relief he had been waiting for did not come. The doctor might still find a reason for the memory gap. He could be afraid of his own condition, agree to tests, go into the hospital. But the camera had seen the red booth. While Asgar was talking about it in the security room, it already existed in a file on someone else's computer.

"Where did the man go?" he asked.

Rauf opened the recording again and brought up the frame before the turn.

"Around this corner."

"I saw that. Where after that?"

"Come with me."

***

Beyond the turn, the corridor ended after a few steps.

Asgar had expected more lights farther on, a door outside, stairs. Instead, his gaze hit the end wall and he walked toward it.

Rauf followed. Katya stayed by the turn; from here Asgar could see her shoulder and part of her face. She was looking in his direction without coming any farther.

"Twelve meters," the investigator said. "From the corner to the end."

Asgar touched the end wall. Ran his hand along the seam, bent toward the floor. The concrete was uneven, stained with paint in places. He looked up, examined the ceiling, then slowly came back along the other wall.

"Was anything here closed off? Blocked?"

"Not when we inspected it."

"A door could have been disguised."

"We checked the walls and the floor plan."

Asgar looked at the end wall again. The man had gone this way. He had stepped around the corner confidently, had not stopped before it. At the time, Asgar had thought he simply knew where he was going.

"What about coming back?"

"He doesn't return on the footage we reviewed."

"How long did you watch after he went in?"

"The whole evening and night. Then spot checks after that. We didn't see this man again."

Asgar turned toward him.

"There's one passage. If he came out, he had to walk past the camera."

"That's why I haven't closed the case."

He spoke calmly, and Asgar wanted to grab him by the shoulder and turn him toward the wall: there it was, the end of the corridor, there was nowhere to go. But Rauf could see the wall perfectly well. He was standing right beside him, had already measured the distance and written it down.

Asgar walked to the end again. He stopped, leaving an arm's length between himself and the concrete. There was enough room behind him to turn around, but he had to force himself to do it. He did not want to lose sight of the wall even for a second.

Katya called:

"Asgar. What are you looking for?"

"I don't know."

He walked toward her. Right at the corner, he stopped and looked back. The empty stretch of corridor was visible in full.

"He would have come back past me," Asgar said. "While I was standing by the booth. I would have seen him."

"While you were standing by the booth—yes. After that, you were gone too."

Asgar wanted to snap at him but held back. Rauf had left the qualification again. Asgar needed at least one place where he could put a full stop and stop doubting.

Katya took his hand.

***

Back in the security room, Rauf let him watch the man moving one more time.

Asgar no longer asked for the face to be enlarged. He watched the man come out, straighten, walk past. Over the short stretch of recording, the man would speed up, then place one leg more carefully. Asgar tried to remember whether he had heard him say anything. Only footsteps. Katya on the mobile. His own voice.

"Have you met him before?" Rauf asked.

"I don't remember anyone like him."

"Did he give you anything? Touch you?"

"No."

"Try to speak to you?"

Asgar shook his head.

On the screen, the man looked back at the door.

"And here," Rauf said, "you decided to look at the booth because he did?"

"Yes. I thought he'd left something there."

Rauf wrote down the answer.

Asgar rubbed a hand over his face. He wanted to add something useful. Height, age, a distinguishing feature. He had been close—closer than the investigator was sitting now. He could have seen. But he had been trying to hear Katya, letting the man pass, looking for quiet. The face had not remained whole in his memory, and every new replay risked replacing what little he still remembered.

"That's enough for now," he said.

Rauf stopped the video.

Katya stood and reached for her bag.

"Can we go?"

The investigator asked them to keep their phones on and clarified where Asgar would be that evening. Asgar gave his home address. Rauf wrote it beside the number and closed the notebook.

At the door, Asgar turned back.

"If you find that man, call me right away."

"I will."

"Even if he says he knows nothing."

Rauf held his gaze.

"You think he knows?"

Asgar looked at the frozen frame. The man was standing by the turn, his head turned toward the red door. Asgar could find nothing else in the image.

"I want to ask him."

They went out into the concourse. Katya walked beside him, holding his arm. The daylight by the exit was bright; from outside came engines and short car horns. Asgar took out his mobile to mute it, then left it as it was.

Near the outer door, he stopped.

"You saw it?"

Katya turned toward him.

"The booth. On the recording. You saw it?"

She tightened her grip on his hand.

"I saw it."

Asgar nodded and stepped outside with her.
