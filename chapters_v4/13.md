Chapter 13. Under the Open Sky

The receiver clicked right beside his ear.

Asgar flinched and pressed it closer.

"I'm here. Can you hear me?"

The answer came through crackling.

"I hear you. The connection is going. Get ready to exit."

He looked through the glass. Pale sky showed above the ruined wall, but below, among the debris, there was deep shadow. Wind carried dust along the ground. No lights in windows, no people.

"Exit where? Everything here is destroyed."

"Describe the area in front of the door."

Asgar leaned down. Beyond the threshold lay a sloping slab, with scattered stones below it. Farther on, the surface looked flatter.

"Concrete. Slight slope. I don't see anyone."

The line crackled again. He caught only the end of the sentence.

"...safe to open. Get out."

"What if I stay?"

"Don't stay. This state is ending. I don't know what will happen inside after that."

Asgar tightened his grip on the receiver. He wanted her to say something else: where he was, how much time there was, where to go after he came out. Her voice was already breaking into short fragments.

He put the receiver down, took the handle, and pulled.

The door opened with a jerk. Cold air struck his face. Asgar hunched his shoulders, looked at his feet, and cautiously shifted his weight onto the slab outside. His sole held.

He stepped out completely.

The wind tugged at the door. Asgar held it, then let go and started down sideways, placing his feet across the slope. Fine gravel crunched under his right boot. His foot slid, and he fell onto one knee, getting a palm down in time.

Pain hit sharply.

"Son of a..."

He froze, breathing through his teeth. Then he lifted his hand from the ground and looked at his trousers. The fabric over his right knee was scraped through, blood showing beneath it.

Asgar got up, holding the edge of the slab.

The booth stood on the slab just above him. He looked up and saw that the red post beside the door had become translucent: the ruined wall was showing through it.

"Wait!"

He jerked upward and stopped when pain shot through his knee.

The red body trembled. The edges of the glass went pale, stones and wall showed through them for an instant—and the booth was gone.

Dust, several stones, and a rectangular patch the wind had not yet covered with grit remained on the slab.

Asgar stared at it until the cold worked its way under his T-shirt.

Then he turned around.

---

A passage ran between two rows of ruined buildings.

Part of the nearest facade remained. Nothing was visible behind the window openings; the upper floors had collapsed inward. Short metal rods stuck from the wall. A strip of faded material hung from one and moved in the wind.

Asgar took several steps, testing his leg. The knee hurt when it bent, but it held. Blood slowly spread beneath the fabric.

He stopped by the wall and listened.

Something hummed far away. The sound would appear, become steadier, then fade again. Asgar could not tell whether it came from above or beyond the buildings.

He took out the phone.

The black glass reflected the sky. Asgar pressed the button; the screen did not come on. Without a working phone, he had no map, no translation, no connection to Martin. He put it away again.

He headed toward where the passage widened.

After a few minutes, he saw wheel tracks. Two dark lines crossed the layer of dust and turned behind a low wall. Between them were shoeprints.

Asgar crouched, bracing a hand against his uninjured knee.

The wind had not yet softened the edges of the tracks.

He straightened and was about to call out when an arm appeared beyond the wall.

The palm was raised toward him.

Asgar froze.

Then a person in dense gray clothing looked out. The lower half of the face was covered with fabric. The person sharply lowered the palm, motioning down, then pointed at the ground beside the wall.

Asgar did not move.

The stranger repeated the gesture and crouched himself.

The hum grew louder.

Asgar lowered himself beside the wall, keeping his weight on the left leg and stretching the right forward. His shoulder pressed against concrete. He held his breath.

A shadow slid over the passage.

Through the gap between the walls, he managed to see a dark body, broad and flat. It moved without visible wheels, keeping above the debris. Something underneath rotated and flashed briefly.

Asgar ducked his head.

The hum slowly moved away.

The stranger stayed motionless for a long time. Then he turned his head, looked along the passage, and beckoned Asgar over.

---

A low cargo cart stood behind the wall. Two closed containers were strapped to it. A woman with closely cropped hair waited by the handle. She held a small device, shifting her gaze between it and Asgar.

The person who had called him over removed the cloth from his face.

The man was younger than he had looked from a distance. Dust had settled in the fold beside his nose; dark stubble covered his chin. He said several words to the woman and pointed in the direction Asgar had come from.

She answered sharply.

Asgar raised his hands, showing empty palms.

"I don't understand."

The woman looked at him.

He repeated it in Azerbaijani, then in English.

The man beside the cart took a flat box from his pocket. He pressed a side button, spoke a short phrase, and held the device toward Asgar.

A distorted voice came from the speaker:

"Speak... more."

"My name is Asgar. I don't know where I am. I need help."

The device hissed. Symbols changed on the small screen.

The woman impatiently pointed at the box. The man answered, shook his head, and scrolled through several lines.

Asgar waited, shivering from the cold.

At last the speaker spoke again:

"Language old. Speak short."

He nodded.

The woman pointed first at his pockets, then at the device in her own hand.

"Transmission?"

"What?"

She repeated it. The translator produced the same word.

Asgar took out the dead phone and showed the dark screen.

"Phone doesn't work."

The man reached toward the phone, but Asgar pulled it back.

He stopped. Pointed at the button, then at the dark glass. Asgar pressed it himself. Nothing happened.

The woman looked at the device in her hand, then at the phone, and gestured: don't turn it on.

Asgar nodded and put the phone away.

"Follow us. Turn nothing on."

Asgar pointed upward.

"What was that?"

The man took the box back; the woman was already pulling the cart by the handle.

"Old security," the device translated. "No transmission—not target. Turn on—it checks. Don't stand here."

They moved along the wall.

Asgar followed.

---

The man stayed at the rear.

When Asgar fell behind, he waited at the next turn. Sometimes he pointed where to place a foot, sometimes made him stop and looked around the corner himself. The woman guided the cart along a narrow strip between debris; the wheels barely rattled.

On one descent, Asgar bent his knee badly and hissed.

The man came over and pointed at the blood.

"Can walk?"

"I can."

He looked at Asgar's face, then offered his forearm. Asgar leaned on it while they descended from the broken threshold.

At the lower wall, the man stopped and touched his own chest.

"Damir."

"Asgar."

"Asgar," he repeated.

The woman looked back at them.

Damir named her:

"Radmila."

She did not repeat the name. She only gestured for them to move faster.

Farther on, the route went beneath a ceiling. The darkness smelled of damp and old metal. Asgar ran a hand along the wall to keep his balance and felt smooth facing beneath his fingers. Pieces of tile lay on the floor.

He tried to understand what the place had once been, but Damir touched his shoulder and pointed ahead.

A weak light burned there.

---

Another person met them at the entrance.

His jacket was open, showing a worn shirt beneath. Seeing Asgar, he immediately asked something. Radmila answered without releasing the cart. The man looked at the bloody knee, then stepped aside to clear the passage.

It was warm beyond the door.

Asgar stopped as soon as he crossed the threshold. Pipes ran along the wall, lamps hung beneath the ceiling inside protective grilles. The clatter of dishes came from a neighboring room. Someone was speaking without raising their voice.

Radmila pointed him toward a bench by the wall.

Asgar sat. His hands were shaking; he hid them between his knees, but had to move the right leg aside immediately.

Damir set the translator beside him.

The man in the open jacket brought water, clean cloth, and a small box.

"Nazar," he introduced himself, touching his chest. Then he pointed at the knee and raised his eyebrows questioningly.

Asgar nodded.

Nazar helped free the abrasion from the fabric stuck to it. The water was cool; Asgar clenched his teeth while the dirt was washed away.

"Stone?" the translator asked.

"Fell."

Nazar examined the abrasion and took a clean strip of material from the box.

"Shallow. Need wash. Then step carefully."

Asgar nodded and lowered his gaze to the cup.

Radmila returned, placed a cup in front of him, and sat opposite.

"Now tell us."

Asgar took the cup in both hands.

"I came out of a red cabin. There, behind the ruined wall. Then it disappeared."

Radmila looked at Damir.

He answered her. They exchanged several phrases without translation, and Asgar was already about to interrupt when Damir pushed the box closer.

"We saw red," he said. "From other side. Went toward place. Then heard security. You came out into passage."

Asgar nodded so quickly he spilled water.

"Yes. I came from there."

Radmila pointed at the cup: put it down.

He obeyed.

"What's inside?"

"A telephone. A woman speaks through it."

"She's inside?"

"No. I've never seen her."

Radmila frowned. The translator repeated her question differently:

"Who else came out?"

"No one. Just me."

Damir leaned against the wall.

Radmila asked where Asgar had come from before the cabin.

He answered briefly: Baku, the year two thousand one hundred ninety-one, a technical complex. Martin and Inessa had stayed there.

"And before that?"

Asgar looked at the translator.

"I entered this cabin in Baku in two thousand fourteen. Then there were other exits. The last one—two thousand one hundred ninety-one."

The translator went silent.

Nazar tapped its edge, waited for a response, and showed Asgar a screen with numbers.

"Year? This?"

"Yes."

Nazar switched something. A second row appeared beside the local symbols. Asgar could read the month names.

The translator spoke with pauses:

"Old calendar. Baku. Comparison."

Asgar looked at the row again.

"Yes. Like that."

Nazar displayed the current date in the same representation.

November third. Two thousand seven hundred forty-four.

Asgar stared at the numbers.

In 2191, four recordings he had intended to finish today remained on the table. Here, that day was five hundred fifty-three years ago.

Someone in the room put dishes on a table. A door slammed in the far passage, and cold air came through from there.

"Baku?" he asked.

Radmila nodded.

He ran a hand over his face.

"What happened here?"

She looked at Nazar, then answered at greater length than before. The translator produced several understandable words: shutdowns, sectors, old systems. Then it stumbled over an unfamiliar term and repeated it.

Asgar raised a hand.

"Wait. I didn't understand."

Radmila tried again in short phrases so the translator would not lose the meaning.

"Old systems remained without common control. Security holds its sectors. Repair repairs. Transport follows old routes. Each has its own rules."

"They rebelled?"

The translator stumbled over the word. Asgar repeated more simply:

"Machines. Against people."

"No one fought," Radmila said. "No one decided anything. Each keeps doing what it always did."

"Then what was that above?"

"The same thing. Only up there they do it on top of one another."

Asgar remembered the dark body above the passage.

"Are they connected to each other?"

"In some places. Here, badly. One system may break what another later restores."

"Why do they attack people?"

"If you enter a closed sector without authorization, to the system you're a violation. Our old permissions died long ago. One will warn. Another strikes immediately."

"Can't you shut them off?"

"Sometimes. Then the neighboring system comes to repair and turns it back on."

Asgar looked at the low ceiling, the lamps, the cup.

"And the whole city is like this?"

"No. Different everywhere. We live between sectors. You'll see tomorrow."

Damir said something brief to her without looking at Asgar. Radmila answered even more briefly.

"What?" Asgar asked.

She pulled the translator toward herself.

"He says: the man came with empty hands. I say: that's obvious."

Asgar set the cup on the table.

"I'll work for it. Tell me what to do."

"I will. For now remember the rule: go only with one of us, turn nothing on, touch nothing without asking. This is not a request."

"Understood."

Radmila took her cup and left through the far passage.

Nazar watched her go.

"We changed route today because of your red thing. With cargo. If security had passed lower, we'd have abandoned the cart and come back empty."

Asgar looked at the door Radmila had disappeared through.

"I didn't ask you to come after me."

"I know. We came anyway."

Nazar moved closer to the translator. He still held the empty dressing box and tapped it against his knee.

"What did you have there? Back then, in your year."

"What do you mean?"

"Just. What did you have. Outside."

Asgar thought.

"That evening—a stadium. Tens of thousands of people sitting and watching twenty-two men fail to hit a ball properly."

Nazar laughed before the translator finished the sentence, then listened to the rest and laughed again.

"Tens of thousands. In one place."

"In one."

He shook his head and went to put the box away.

---

They gave him a jacket.

Nazar brought two, held one against Asgar's shoulders, shook his head, and left the other. The sleeves were slightly short, but the fastening closed.

"Ours," he said, pointing at the jacket. "You can wear. When leave—tell."

Asgar nodded.

The translator remained on the table. Without it, the speech around him became separate sounds again. Asgar understood gestures, directions of gaze, sometimes a questioning intonation. The rest had to be guessed, and he quickly stopped answering until he was sure.

After the meal, Damir took him to a small room with two sleeping platforms. Folded blankets occupied one. The man moved them, checked the light by the door, and showed Asgar where to lie down.

Then he touched the box he had brought with him.

"Talk in morning. Rest now."

"The booth may come back."

Damir listened to the translation.

"Where?"

"I don't know. Before, it was nearby. Last time—far away."

"If you hear or see it, call us. Don't go alone."

Asgar sat on the edge of the bed.

"Will you look for it?"

"We'll check cameras. The ones that still work."

Damir took the translator and went out.

Asgar remained alone.

He carefully stretched out the right leg. It burned beneath the bandage, but blood was no longer showing through. The phone lay in the jacket pocket; Asgar moved it under the edge of the folded blanket, then immediately took it back out and left it near his head.

Sleep would not come.

He remembered the room with moving panels beyond the window. There, he did not have to listen for what was flying over the roof. He could go out for coffee, choose the wrong cup size, go back, and correct it.

Asgar reached toward the phone near his head.

He wanted to write Martin that he had made it. That he was alive. That there were people here too.

He pressed the button. The black glass did not change. Asgar left the phone where it was.

Twice during the night, people passed beyond the wall. Once they stopped by the door. Asgar held his breath, but the footsteps soon moved away.

Toward morning, he fell asleep.

---

Radmila woke him.

She stood in the doorway with a lamp in her hand. She said something, pointed into the corridor, and waited for him to sit up.

Asgar pulled on the jacket and checked the phone. He stood carefully. The knee hurt more after being still, but after several steps it eased.

Damir and Nazar were already sitting in the common room.

A screen lay on the table. Radmila put the translator beside it.

"We found the red cabin."

Asgar came over so quickly he hit his thigh against the edge of the bench.

The image was still. It showed a concrete canopy, a dark opening beyond it, and the booth standing at the edge of steps. The upper body was partly hidden beneath the canopy.

"When?"

"This morning," Nazar answered. "Camera transmitted recording. Then they closed the connection."

"Why?"

Radmila pointed at the screen.

"Camera only watches. Sends no requests there. Image taken by wire."

Asgar frowned and looked at the screen again. Damir slid over a second sheet—a rough diagram drawn on dense material.

"We're here."

He put a finger at the lower edge.

"Cabin here. Almost two kilometers."

Asgar traced the distance with his eyes.

"Can we get through?"

Damir did not answer immediately.

A double line ran between the two points on the diagram. He traced it with his fingernail, then pointed at the image of the canopy.

"It's beyond the old perimeter."

Asgar remembered the shadow over the passage. The hum that had made Damir force him down to the ground.

"The same kind of machines there?"

"Yes."

He looked at the red door.

Yesterday he had entered it beside Martin. Now it stood less than two kilometers away, and those two kilometers could not simply be walked.

Asgar pulled the bench closer and sat at the table.

"Show me where we can go."
