Chapter 8. On Both Sides of the Glass

The employee raised the radio to his face.

"Stop the descent. No one into the hall for now."

Asgar backed toward the ramp, watching the far wall. The red was already gone. The yellow stripe lay straight, the concrete beyond it remained gray, but he kept staring until the heel of his shoe caught a chair leg.

The coordinator took the screen from the table.

"Asgar, over here."

There was a door to the control room to the right of the ramp. The employee opened it, let the woman through, then held it for Asgar. He went in last.

The room smelled of plastic and long-cold coffee. A table with several monitors stood along the transparent partition; one had not yet been switched on. From here they could see almost the entire hall except the section right by the ramp. Asgar walked up to the glass.

"It may appear again," he said.

The employee stopped by the door.

"You said the tripod leg was cut off. Where was it standing?"

"Where the booth appeared afterward. I can't tell you more precisely."

The employee looked into the hall, toward the yellow stripe by the far wall.

"Then I can't tell you where it'll appear here either. So far the changes have only been in the hall. We'll keep it empty. You stay here; we'll move everyone else into the corridor."

"Has Katya arrived?"

"I'll find out."

He went out, leaving the door open. The coordinator put the screen on the table and stepped toward the opposite wall. Several times Asgar caught her looking at him; each time she shifted her gaze into the hall.

He took out his mobile. Pressed the button, already knowing nothing would happen, and set the phone beside the dead monitor.

A few minutes later, footsteps sounded outside the door. Asgar turned.

Katya stopped in the doorway.

On the screen he had seen only her face and shoulders. Now she stood there in full, a bag over one bent arm, wearing a light blouse he had never seen. She was looking at him, and Asgar could not understand why he wasn't walking toward her. There were only a few steps between them.

Katya set the bag on the floor. He managed to say:

"Wait, in here..."

She came over and took his face in her hands. Her fingers were cool. Katya turned his head slightly to one side and ran her thumb over his cheek. Asgar stood still while she examined him. Then her hands slid to his shoulders and she pressed herself against him.

He hugged her too hard. Katya let out a short breath into his neck but did not pull away. Asgar loosened his arms, felt the fabric of her blouse under his palm, a fastener, the movement of her back. Her hair smelled different. It made him want to pull her closer until everything became familiar again.

"You're warm," she said.

He tried to answer and only nodded. Katya held him tighter.

The employee said something quietly by the door. The coordinator went out with him. Asgar heard them stop in the corridor. The door remained open.

At last Katya pulled back just enough to look at him.

"Give me your hand."

He held out his right hand. She took it in both of hers and smoothed his fingers flat.

"All the way here I kept thinking I'd arrive and it would turn out to be some kind of mistake."

"Me too."

"No. At least you knew you were here."

Asgar lowered his eyes to her hands. Thin veins showed on one of them. He remembered that hand without them. Katya noticed where he was looking.

"You'll get used to it."

"Katya..."

"I didn't get used to it right away either."

She did not let go of his hand.

Asgar wanted to kiss her. He leaned in, stopped halfway through the movement. Katya moved closer herself and kissed him on the lips. Briefly, carefully. Then she rested her forehead against his chin.

"Please don't look at me like that."

"Like what?"

"Like you're trying to remember."

He hugged her again.

---

The red edges did not appear again. A duty team came down into the hall, set equipment near the entrance, and ran a cable along the wall. Asgar and Katya stayed in the control room until the employee asked him to go into the hall for observation.

Katya stood up at once with him.

"Stay here," the employee said. "So far the changes have only been in the hall, and I don't want to test the distance on you."

Asgar picked up his mobile from the table.

"I'll be right opposite."

He went out, closed the door behind him, and walked up to the partition from the other side. Katya stood by the glass. Her lips were moving, but he could not hear the words.

Asgar pointed to his ear. The employee leaned over the desk in the control room. A speaker by the door clicked.

"Can you hear me now?" Katya asked.

"Yes."

She looked at the small microphone in front of her, then back at him.

"At least sit down. You're standing all the time."

One of the chairs was still by the partition. Asgar pulled it over and sat. Katya settled opposite him behind the glass, her bag on her knees.

For a while they talked like that.

She said she had contacted the sons. The younger wanted to come immediately, but Katya had asked him to wait for her call.

"And the older one?"

"He's not in the city. He's already on his way." Katya looked at her device. "He'll be here in the morning."

Asgar nodded.

Morning was only a few hours away—in the person who was driving toward the city now along a darkening road.

"Show me to them," Asgar said.

"I will. I don't want them to see you for one second, then something happens here and the connection drops. I barely made it here myself."

He looked into the depth of the hall.

"All right."

Katya opened her bag, found a tissue, and wiped beneath her eyes. Then she took out water.

"Have you eaten today?"

Asgar remembered the cold coffee in the yard.

"Not much."

"I see."

She called the employee over. After a while, food was brought to Asgar in paper packaging. He ate sitting by the glass and listened to Katya tell him about the drive there. The navigation system had led her to a locked gate. She drove around the building, ended up at the same gate again, called the coordinator, and only then found the entrance.

"I tell them, everything's closed here. They say, no, it's open. I can see the gate," she said. "Turned out we were talking about different sides of the building."

Asgar smiled with his mouth full. Katya smiled too and immediately looked away, screwing the cap back onto the bottle.

He finished eating. Folded the paper so the crumbs stayed inside.

Outside above them, it gradually grew dark; he could tell from the entrance camera on one of the monitors. Down here the light did not change. Asgar stood several times, walked along the partition, then came back. Katya watched him. Each time he sat down, she continued the conversation from where she had left off.

The whole evening could have passed like that.

The coordinator came to the desk in the control room and showed the employee her screen. Through the open intercom Asgar heard the word "journalists" and raised his head.

"What's going on?"

The woman turned the screen toward the glass. The image showed Asgar standing in the courtyard of the service center with a police officer beside him. Then she opened the old missing-person notice with his photograph.

"A newsroom sent this. They're asking whether it's you. They found the old notice after footage from outside the center was posted. I've asked them to route requests through the unit's reception office."

Katya moved closer to the screen.

"Don't give anyone the children's numbers. I'll call them and warn them."

"We haven't," the coordinator said, then stepped away to answer a call.

Asgar looked at the picture until the screen went dark. Katya called his name; he sat opposite her again.

He started asking about her apartment. Which floor. Whether there was a balcony. Katya said the balcony was small, but at least the morning sun did not hit it directly. Asgar pictured a chair, a mug, her bare feet on the cool floor.

"And where would I..." he began, then stopped.

Katya looked up.

"We'll figure it out at home."

---

Her device signaled while she was putting the bottle back into her bag.

Katya looked at the screen and did not pick it up right away.

"It's the younger one."

Asgar said nothing. After what she had explained, he could not ask.

She turned partly away and answered. Through the partition Asgar could hear only her: that she was here, that it was all true, that no, he should not come tonight. Then she fell silent and listened for a long time.

"Wait," she said into the device. And, without turning her head: "Voice only. He won't look."

She placed the device on the table closer to the partition.

At first there was silence.

Asgar moved closer. He prepared himself to hear the boy who breathed into the microphone and asked whether there was a faucet at the depot. The voice that came through was adult, even, a little lower than Katya's, and completely unfamiliar.

"Dad?"

Asgar swallowed.

"Yes."

The other end went quiet again.

"I don't know what to say."

"Neither do I."

Asgar looked at Katya. She sat with her eyes lowered to the table.

"I watched that stadium recording so many times," his son said. "There's no sound. What could you hear there?"

"The stands were yelling so loudly I couldn't hear your mother," Asgar said. "That's why I went into that corridor. So I could hear her."

His son did not answer.

Asgar waited. He wanted to ask everything at once: what he did for work, where he lived, whether he had children, whether he remembered his tenth birthday. Every question, once spoken aloud, turned into the question of a stranger.

"I spent twenty years trying to understand why you went in there," his son finally said. "It wasn't written anywhere."

"Now it is."

His son let out a short breath—not quite a laugh, but close.

Katya raised a hand toward the device and stopped before touching it.

"I have to go," he said.

"Yes."

The pause was short and noticeable.

"Goodbye," his son said.

And, just before disconnecting:

"Dad."

Katya took the device and put it into her bag without looking at the screen.

Asgar sat with his palms on his knees.

Nothing had changed beyond the partition. Someone in the control room moved a chair.

---

The booth appeared while Asgar was carrying the empty food wrapper toward the door.

First, the reflection of a column broke on a smooth metal panel near the far wall. The vertical line shifted as though someone had moved the lower half of the image. Asgar stopped.

The red body emerged about thirty meters away.

Its lower part sank into the concrete. A beam passed over the door; from where Asgar stood, it looked as though the door would not have enough room to open. For several seconds the edge of the roof doubled. The doubling stretched along the beam in both directions and ended roughly ten meters from the body; beyond that the metal looked normal. Then the nearer section became still too.

Something clicked at the base.

A small gray fragment rolled across the concrete. It stopped at the yellow line.

Asgar set the wrapper on the nearest chair.

Behind the glass, the employee was speaking rapidly into the radio. Katya stood up. He saw her approach the partition, but he kept looking at the fragment.

A telephone rang inside the booth.

"Asgar, stay where you are," came through the speaker.

He nodded. The ringing repeated.

The employee turned one of the monitors toward a colleague. They looked from the image to the hall and back. Asgar could not make out their conversation: the intercom was silent.

At last the speaker came on.

"Everything is stable right now. But I still wouldn't approach yet."

"I'll pick up the receiver. From outside, like before," Asgar said, walking toward the glass.

Katya looked at him.

"Then you'll come back here?"

"Yes."

She held her gaze on the booth.

"And you'll tell me everything they say."

Asgar nodded and crossed the hall.

The closer he got, the more he wanted to turn back. A fresh pale gouge was visible in the concrete floor near the body. The red wall crossed its edge. Asgar went around the place on the right and stopped before the door.

The handle was warm.

He pulled the door toward himself. It passed beneath the beam even though a second earlier it had looked certain to hit it. Asgar looked up, then at his own hand, and forced himself to focus on the telephone.

He lifted the receiver and stayed in front of the threshold.

"I came out in two thousand thirty-nine."

"Asgar, I hear you."

The same voice. Asgar tightened his grip on the receiver.

"Twenty-five years. Do you understand?"

"Yes. Minutes passed for you. Here—twenty-five years."

Asgar adjusted the receiver against his ear. Across the hall he could see Katya—a small figure behind the transparent partition.

"I found my wife," he said. "She's here. I want to stay."

The woman did not answer immediately.

"You can stay. I won't force you to enter."

"And what happens to this? To the appearances?"

"The connection remains. The cabin may appear again. Where and when, I don't know."

"The concrete chipped here. Right by the body."

"Stay away from the floor boundary."

Asgar moved one foot a little farther back.

"I have nowhere to get away from you. I can't spend the rest of my life living on an empty concrete floor."

"Without your entry, we haven't closed it."

"And where will you close it?"

He waited for the familiar I don't know. The woman said:

"To work with an incomplete transition, we need the source."

Asgar frowned.

"What source?"

"The point the module was sent from and where it can be received."

"The module is this cabin?"

"Yes."

He looked at the telephone, at the round metal dial beneath the set.

"So the place where it was made?"

"Not necessarily. It was sent from there, and it can be received back there."

"Are there people there who can send me home?"

"I don't know whether they'll send you home."

"But I can ask them?"

"If we reach the source, at least they'll be able to handle your return separately. I don't have a solution now."

Asgar said nothing. His fingers on the door handle were beginning to go numb.

"I need the year two thousand fourteen," he said. "Not some other good year. Mine."

"That date can't be selected."

"But you can select the source?"

"No. The next stop isn't selected at all. I don't know when the source will come up."

Behind the glass, Katya shifted position. Asgar could not tell what she was doing and craned his neck as if that might help.

"If I stay, will you keep looking for a way?"

"Yes. But without continuing the transition, the connection did not close today."

He stood there listening to the faint noise on the line.

Then he said he would step away to speak with his wife, put the receiver back on the cradle, and carefully let the door close.

---

On the way back to the partition, Asgar was already choosing his words.

He wanted to say that at least there was a possibility there. That here they would only be waiting for the next appearance. That they could not leave everything as it was after what had happened to the tripod leg and the concrete.

Katya was waiting at the glass, standing.

"What did she say?"

He told her. About the source, about the point of departure, about being unable to choose a year. Katya listened without interrupting. The building employee stood beside her.

"And if you stay?" she asked.

"They'll keep working. But they can't close the connection yet."

Katya turned to the employee.

"What can you do?"

He pulled the microphone closer so Asgar could hear too.

"Keep him here. Close the hall, provide separate accommodation, arrange visits with family. If something changes, we'll adjust the measures. I can't say everything will stop."

"How long here?" Asgar asked.

"You're here today. Tomorrow we'll assess the situation and decide together."

"And the documents?" Katya looked at the coordinator behind him.

"We'll sort out the documents. His identity is already confirmed."

Asgar listened and looked at Katya's bag by the table.

She could bring him clothes tomorrow. The day after, the sons could come. He could watch them walk into this room, hear their adult voices. He could wake up in the morning and learn that nothing had happened during the night.

"I can come tomorrow," Katya said. "And in the evening. As often as needed. You don't have to come home today."

"Katya, I haven't decided anything yet."

She looked at him more closely.

"Then why are you answering as if I'm trying to talk you into something impossible?"

"I'm just explaining that this won't end on its own."

"I heard. And the people beside me heard. They're offering a way forward."

Asgar looked back at the booth. Katya fell silent. When he turned back, she was standing closer to the glass.

"You already want to go."

"I want this to end."

"So do I. Answer what I actually said."

He rubbed a hand over the back of his neck.

"If there's a chance to get to the place where they can deal with this..."

"Then you want to go."

Asgar looked at the employee beside her. The man stepped back toward the desk but did not leave the room.

"I'm still here," Asgar said. "We're talking."

"You're listing all the reasons you have to leave. And when I suggest you stay at least for the night, you say you haven't decided."

He felt heat rise into his face.

"Because we have no proof it'll be safe here tomorrow."

"And you have no proof they'll send you back there."

"The concrete already chipped here."

"I saw."

"If that happens beside you..."

Katya stepped back from the glass. Asgar stopped. She ran a hand across her mouth and waited until she could speak.

"Don't do that again—don't make it so I have to send you away myself. I can't say there's no danger. But I also can't say 'go.' I want you to stay. I just held you."

He looked at her.

"I know."

"No. For you, we were still talking this morning. But I..."

She did not finish. Picked up the bottle from the desk, set it back down unopened.

Asgar lowered his head. The reflection along the bottom edge of the partition showed his shoes and the chair legs. He could sit down. Ask for more food. Say he wasn't going anywhere until morning. For several seconds there was nothing in his mind but the chair, the food, Katya behind the glass, and a night without another ringing telephone.

Then he imagined morning, the phone ringing again, and himself once more explaining to Katya why he needed to approach it.

"I was already leaning toward going," he said. "When I came back from the phone."

Katya nodded once.

"That's what you should have said."

"I was afraid."

"I'm afraid too, Asgar. But I'm not telling you I don't care whether you stay or go."

Asgar looked up at her.

"I do care."

"I know," Katya said.

---

They were silent for a long time.

The staff in the control room spoke quietly among themselves; the intercom carried only indistinct background sound. Katya sat with her hands resting on her bag. Asgar stood by the glass. He wanted to ask her to come closer but wasn't sure he had the right to ask anything of her now.

At last she stood up on her own.

"What will you take?"

He patted his pocket.

"Phone. Documents."

"The phone is dead."

"I know."

Katya opened her mouth, but instead of answering she nodded.

"There are photos on it."

"Yes."

Asgar took out the mobile. Her face reflected in the black glass on the other side of the partition.

"I won't lose them."

Katya closed her eyes. He put the phone away.

"That's not what you should have said."

She did not answer. Asgar placed his palm on the partition. Katya looked at it, then raised her own. The thickness of the glass lay between their fingers. He could not feel her warmth.

"I love you," he said.

Her mouth trembled.

"I love you too. That's why I'm asking you to stay."

Asgar stood without moving his hand. Katya lowered hers first.

The employee at the desk waited without suggesting anything. Behind him was the door to the corridor, then the building exit, the city, Katya's car. Asgar looked that way, then back at her.

"I'm going."

Katya tightened her grip on the strap of her bag. Asgar waited, but she did not answer. Then he turned toward the hall.

---

At the booth Asgar stopped and looked back.

Katya remained by the partition. He could barely make out her face now. Only the light blouse, the dark line of her hair, the hand holding the edge of the desk.

He opened the door and lifted the receiver.

"I'm here."

"I hear you."

Asgar wanted to ask how long the next transition would take. Instead he looked at the threshold.

"There are people outside. They're staying."

"Understood. Step fully inside before closing the door."

He stepped over the red edge, brought the other foot in. Turned inside, shifted the receiver so the cord would not lie in the opening. With his free hand he took hold of the inner handle.

Katya was visible through the open gap. Asgar paused. Behind the partition she raised her head. He did not know whether she could see his face or only his figure in the doorway.

He wanted to go back if only to say it differently. Without explanations, without trying to win her agreement.

He pulled the door. It moved heavily. Asgar kept his eyes on the light blouse through two layers of glass until the door seated in the frame.

"Closed," he said.

He let go of the inner handle.

Something clicked in the receiver.

Katya remained where she was.

Then the line of the partition shifted. The edge of the desk stretched, passed through her figure; Asgar jerked forward and struck the booth glass with his palm.

The hall disappeared.

He stood with his hand pressed to the cold surface and looked at daylight outside.

---

Katya was gripping the edge of the desk. Where the red door had been a moment earlier, only concrete remained. The employee beside her called someone on the radio and reached for the intercom; the light by the microphone went out.

She was still staring into the depth of the hall when the straight line on the floor bent.

At first Katya thought the glass had trembled. Then the yellow stripe on the concrete shifted relative to the wall, and the section of the hall beyond it seemed to shorten. The table by the far wall stretched sideways and immediately returned to place. The employee beside her fell silent.

In the empty place where no one had been a second earlier, a human figure began to appear.

He did not step out of a booth—there was no booth there anymore. The man emerged directly in the hall, as if several mismatched pieces of space had finally fitted together around him. He stood sideways with one arm extended as though he had just tried to brace against a wall. Then he looked around sharply.

Katya recognized the face before she understood anything else.

His cheeks were hollow. His hair had grown longer. His clothes were different.

"Asgar?"

He saw her.

Katya knew it from how instantly his face changed. Asgar shouted something and ran toward the control room. She turned to the door and pressed the device beside the opening.

"Open it. He's back."

The employee was already at the desk. The door began sliding aside.

Asgar almost reached the pale line on the floor. He crossed it with his right foot and swayed as though the step was not where he expected. He stopped and looked down. Katya pressed her palm to the glass.

He raised a hand toward her.

His mouth moved again. The speaker was silent.

Katya pulled the door toward herself. Asgar stepped along the pale line, and the floor between them stretched. She did not see it at first: he was moving toward her, but the distance was not shrinking. Then it grew.

"Here, Asgar. I'm opening it, wait."

He stopped and reached out. Katya held the edge of the door and reached toward him with her free hand even though there were still several meters between them.

Asgar moved again.

The pale line split in two. Through the concrete near his feet appeared a different pale floor, then the edge of a table. Asgar turned sideways, trying to go around it.

The table shifted with him.

Katya slapped her palm against the glass.

Asgar lunged toward her and fell forward with one arm outstretched. For a moment his figure stretched together with the changing hall, then a white wall of another room showed through his shoulder.

"Asgar!"

He was still looking at her.

Then he vanished.

The floor became level again. The yellow stripe returned to its place. Beyond the partition was the same empty technical hall, and for several more seconds Katya kept her arm extended toward the opening.
