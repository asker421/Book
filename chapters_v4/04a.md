Interlude I. Deviation

The woman at the console was checking the completion of another reconciliation. The door had already closed; all that remained was to receive the result for the contents.

Instead, a discrepancy line appeared.

She opened the measurements, expecting to find a missed segment, but the data kept arriving. There was mass inside that did not exist in the calculation.

She requested a repeat. While the response assembled, signs of breathing appeared beside the discrepancy. The woman leaned toward the console, cleared the previous results from the shared panel, and made room for a second sensor set.

“I have a person inside.”

The engineer at the next desk stood up. She showed him the respiration and motor activity; both confirmed on the repeat. He ran the sequence backward to the moment the door sealed, then returned to the current data.

The woman initiated a call to the booth.

The unit was unavailable.

“Stop the return.”

The engineer sent the abort command. The request was accepted, but no execution confirmation followed. While he checked the response, the woman called the booth again. She needed to tell the person not to touch anything. She had already moved the microphone to her mouth even though no connection existed yet.

Corwin arrived with the general alarm and stopped behind the engineer.

“What happened after abort?”

“Didn’t execute. I’m checking hold.”

The woman kept watching the respiration trace. A gap appeared in the data; she stepped back one measurement, saw the previous value, and forced herself to reopen the current one.

It had not arrived yet.

The engineer sent the hold command. On the adjacent panel, the discrepancy region widened.

“Release hold,” Corwin said. “Keep observation running.”

“There’s a person in there,” the woman said. “He’s locked in.”

“Do not open the door now. The boundary is moving.”

The engineer was already releasing the hold. She waited for the measurements at least to stop disappearing. The next respiration marker appeared separately from the rest of the data. Then the external environment updated, and the time on the two data streams no longer matched.

The woman gave the engineer control of the shared panel. He separated the streams, checked their origin, and waited until the measurements could be compared again.

The booth still showed signs of life.

External time had advanced by four days.

“Four?” she asked.

The engineer showed her both clocks. For the person inside, there had been only a brief transition.

“Four days,” he repeated, already recording it. “Does he have anyone out there?”

“I don’t know who he has.”

She was still staring at the difference when the call finally connected. Something clicked in the receiver, and a man said loudly that he was locked in and the door would not open.

“Can you hear me?”

He could. He demanded to be let out.

The woman asked whether he could stand and whether he was injured. He said he could stand, then immediately asked where everyone had gone.

She turned toward the engineer.

“Check the lock.”

He began working on the boundary. The woman told the man there had been a malfunction, said she had a link to the booth. He asked whether she was at the stadium, who had called her. He spoke quickly, kept returning to the full stadium, to the door he had entered so he could make a phone call.

She managed to ask his name.

Asgar.

He needed to get out; everything else irritated him.

The engineer confirmed that opening was permissible and released the lock. The woman asked about the handle. Asgar said it turned. The door state changed.

Now they could find out what had happened before it closed.

“How did you get inside?”

“Through the door. A man came out, then I went in. What is this—”

“Another person?”

The engineer looked up.

The woman listened to the answer while already studying the measurements from before closure. A gray-haired man had come out before Asgar. She asked whether he had actually seen the man leave the booth.

Asgar confirmed, annoyed.

Corwin came to the console. The woman turned to him and managed to tell him about the second person; by the time she faced the microphone again, the link was gone. The cradle switch clicked in the receiver. She initiated another call, but the voice did not return.

Asgar left the booth.

The door closed behind him.

The measurements showed no living mass remaining inside. The woman kept the completion line open while the engineer verified the exit.

The booth lost its external state.

The emergency record continued.

“He is definitely outside?”

“Yes. Exit confirmed.”

She switched the call system to reconnect.

No time indication appeared.

Nothing changed on the completion line.

***

After the second conversation, the woman logged Asgar’s refusal to enter. He intended to leave the home where his children were staying. The next possible contact was estimated at approximately forty-seven hours of his external time.

She left the interval open. There was no other way to reach him.

The engineer called her to his desk. He was still working through the first event. Two source streams stood on the panel; both contained a faint trace highlighted near the outer boundary.

The woman looked first at the door-closure marker.

The trace came earlier.

“This is before Asgar?”

“Before registration. The second source retained it too.”

She asked him to show the full interval. The engineer removed the zoom. The earlier deviation nearly vanished among the rest of the data. Without his marker, she would not have found it herself.

“The man?”

“I’m not even sure it’s movement. I’d need a different authorization for an active test.”

The woman opened a request. Corwin, still standing near her console, came over to read the conditions. When he saw the selected mode, he returned control to her.

“Use the records. We are not interfering with the process over this.”

The woman withdrew the intervention request. The engineer saved both streams, and she attached the relevant section of the first conversation without merging the records.

Asgar’s voice ended on an irritated confirmation: yes, he had seen the man himself.

She returned to her station and opened the preparation for the next call.

First they needed to know where he would be waiting and who would be nearby.

Below that, she left the question about the man at the door.

Until the next connection, there was no one to ask.
