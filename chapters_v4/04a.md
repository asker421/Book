Interlude I. Deviation

The woman at the console was checking the completion of another verification cycle. The door had already closed; all that remained was to receive the contents report. Instead, a discrepancy line appeared. She opened the measurements, expecting to find a missed segment, but the data kept coming. There was mass inside that had not been included in the calculation.

She requested a repeat. While the response was being assembled, signs of breathing appeared beside the discrepancy. The woman pulled closer to the console, cleared the previous results from the shared panel, and made room for a second sensor set.

"I have a person inside."

The engineer at the neighboring desk stood up. She showed him the breathing and motor activity; both were confirmed on the repeat. He ran the sequence backward to the moment the door sealed, then returned to the current data. The woman initiated a call to the cabin. The device was unavailable.

"Stop the return."

The engineer sent the cancellation. The request was accepted, but no confirmation of execution followed. While he checked the response, the woman called the cabin again. She needed to tell the person not to touch anything. She had already pulled the microphone toward her mouth even though there was still no connection.

Korvin arrived in response to the general alarm and stopped behind the engineer.

"What happened after the cancellation?"

"It didn't take. I'm checking the hold."

The woman watched the breathing. A gap appeared in the data; she went back one measurement, saw the previous value, and forced herself to open the current one again. It had not arrived yet. The engineer applied the hold. On the adjacent panel, the discrepancy area expanded.

"Release the hold," Korvin said. "Keep observation running."

"There's a person in there," the woman said. "He's locked in."

"Don't open the door now. The boundary is moving."

The engineer was already releasing the hold. She waited, hoping at least the measurements would stop dropping out afterward. The next breathing marker appeared separately from the rest of the data. Then the external environment updated, and the time values in the two rows no longer matched.

The woman gave the engineer control of the shared panel. He separated the rows, checked their origin, and waited until the measurements could be compared again. Signs of life remained inside the cabin. External time had moved forward by four days.

"Four?" she asked again.

The engineer showed her both readings. For the person inside, only a short transition had passed.

"Four days," he repeated, already recording it. "Does he have anyone out there?"

"I don't know who he has."

She was still looking at the difference when the call finally connected. There was a click in the receiver, and a man said loudly that he was locked in, that the door would not open.

"Can you hear me?"

He could. He demanded to be let out. The woman asked whether he could stand, whether he was injured. He said he could and immediately asked where everyone had gone.

She turned to the engineer.

"Check the lock."

He dealt with the boundary. The woman told the man there had been a malfunction and that she was maintaining contact with the cabin. He asked whether she was at the stadium, who had called her. He spoke quickly, kept coming back to the full stadium, to the door he had entered to make a call. She managed to ask his name. Asgar. He needed to get out; everything else irritated him.

The engineer confirmed that opening was permissible and released the lock. The woman asked about the handle. Asgar said it turned. The state of the door changed. Now they could determine what had happened before it closed.

"How did you get inside?"

"Through the door. A man came out, I went in. What is this..."

"Another person?"

The engineer raised his head. The woman listened to the answer, already looking at the measurements from before the seal. A man, gray-haired, had come out before Asgar. She asked whether he had actually seen the man exit the cabin. Asgar irritably confirmed that he had.

Korvin came to the console. The woman turned to him and managed to mention the second person; when she returned to the microphone, the connection had already dropped. The receiver clicked. She initiated the call again, but the voice did not return.

Asgar exited. The door closed behind him. The measurements showed that no living mass remained inside. The woman kept the completion line in front of her while the engineer checked the exit. The cabin lost its external state, while the emergency recording continued.

"He's definitely outside?"

"Yes. The exit is confirmed."

She switched the call to reconnection mode. No time indication appeared. Nothing changed in the completion line.

***

After the second conversation, the woman logged Asgar's refusal to enter. He intended to leave the home where his children remained. The next opportunity for contact was estimated at roughly forty-seven hours of his external time. She left that interval open: there was no other way to contact him.

The engineer called her to his desk. He was still analyzing the first event. Two source rows were displayed on the panel; in both, a faint trace near the external boundary was highlighted.

The woman first looked at the door-closing marker. The trace came earlier.

"This is before Asgar?"

"Before registration. The second source preserved it too."

She asked him to show the full interval. The engineer removed the zoom. The earlier deviation nearly disappeared among the rest of the data. If he had not left a marker, she would not have found it herself.

"That man?"

"I'm not even sure it's movement. We'd need a different authorization for an active check."

The woman opened the request. Korvin, still standing by her console, came over to read the conditions. When he saw the selected mode, he returned control to her.

"Use the recordings. We don't interfere with the process for this."

The woman withdrew the intervention request. The engineer saved both rows, and she attached the segment from the first conversation without merging the records. Asgar's voice cut off on an irritated confirmation: yes, he had seen it himself.

She returned to her station and opened preparation for the next call. First they needed to find out where he was waiting and who was nearby. Below that, she left the question about the man by the door. Until the next connection, there was no one to ask.
