Interlude I. Deviation

The woman at the console was checking the completion of another verification cycle. The door had already closed; all that remained was to receive the result for the contents. Instead, a discrepancy line appeared. She opened the measurements, expecting to find a missing segment, but the data kept coming in. There was mass inside that had not been included in the calculation.

She requested a repeat. While the response was being assembled, signs of breathing appeared beside the discrepancy. The woman moved closer to the console, cleared the previous results from the shared panel, and made room for a second set of sensors.

"I have a person inside."

The engineer at the neighboring desk stood up. She showed him the breathing and motor activity; both indicators were confirmed on the repeat. He ran the sequence back to the moment the door sealed, then returned to the current data. The woman initiated a call to the cabin. The device was unavailable.

"Stop the return."

The engineer submitted a cancellation. The request was accepted, but no confirmation of execution followed. While he checked the response, the woman called the cabin again. She needed to tell the person not to touch anything. She had already brought the microphone toward her mouth even though there was still no connection.

Korvin arrived in response to the general alarm and stopped behind the engineer.

"What happened after the cancellation?"

"It didn't work. I'm checking the hold."

The woman watched the breathing. A gap appeared in the data; she went back one measurement, saw the previous value, and forced herself to open the current one again. It had not arrived yet. The engineer applied the hold. On the adjacent panel, the discrepancy region expanded.

"Release the hold," Korvin said. "Keep observation active."

"There's a person in there," the woman said. "He's locked in."

"Don't open the door now. The boundary is shifting."

The engineer was already releasing the hold. She waited for the measurements at least to stop disappearing afterward. The next breathing mark appeared separately from the rest of the data. Then the external environment updated, and the time values in the two rows no longer matched.

The woman yielded control of the shared panel to the engineer. He separated the rows, checked their origins, and waited until the measurements could be compared again. Signs of life remained in the cabin. External time had advanced by four days.

"Four?" she asked again.

The engineer showed her both readings. For the person inside, only a short transition had passed.

"Four days," he repeated, already recording it. "Does he have anyone out there?"

"I don't know who he has."

She was still looking at the difference when the call finally connected. The receiver clicked, and a man loudly said that he was locked in, that the door would not open.

"Can you hear me?"

He could. He demanded to be let out. The woman asked whether he could stand and whether he was injured. He answered that he could, then immediately asked where everyone had gone.

She turned to the engineer.

"Check the lock."

He began working with the boundary. The woman told the man there had been a malfunction, said that she was maintaining contact with the cabin. He asked whether she was at the stadium, who had called her. He spoke quickly, kept returning to the full stadium, to the door he had stepped behind to make a call. She managed to ask his name. Asgar. He needed to get out; everything else irritated him.

The engineer confirmed that opening was permissible and released the lock. The woman asked about the handle. Asgar answered that it turned. The state of the door changed. Now they could find out what had happened before it closed.

"How did you get inside?"

"Through the door. A man came out, I went in. What is this..."

"Another person?"

The engineer looked up. The woman listened to the answer while already looking at the measurements from before the seal. A man, gray-haired, had come out before Asgar. She asked again whether he had personally seen the man exit the cabin. Asgar confirmed irritably that he had.

Korvin came over to the console. The woman turned to him, managed to tell him about the second person; when she returned to the microphone, the connection had already dropped. The receiver clicked at the cradle. She initiated the call again, but the voice did not return.

Asgar exited. The door closed behind him. The measurements showed that there was no longer any living mass inside. The woman kept the completion line in front of her while the engineer verified the exit. The cabin lost its external state, while the emergency recording continued.

"He's definitely outside?"

"Yes. There is an exit."

She switched the call to reconnection mode. No time indication appeared. Nothing changed in the completion line.

***

After the second conversation, the woman recorded Asgar's refusal to enter. He intended to leave the home where his children remained. The next opportunity for contact was estimated at roughly forty-seven hours of his external time. She left that interval open: there was no other way to contact him.

The engineer called her over to his desk. He was still analyzing the first event. Two source rows were displayed on the panel; in both, a faint trace near the external boundary was highlighted.

The woman first looked at the door-closure marker. The trace came earlier.

"Is this before Asgar?"

"Before registration. The second source retained it too."

She asked him to show the entire interval. The engineer removed the magnification. The earlier deviation nearly disappeared among the rest of the data. If he had not left a marker on it, she would not have found it herself.

"The man?"

"I'm not even sure it's movement. We need different authorization for an active check."

The woman opened a request. Korvin, still standing by her console, came over to read the conditions. Seeing the selected mode, he handed control back to her.

"Use the recordings. We don't interfere with the process for this."

The woman withdrew the request for intervention. The engineer saved both rows, and she attached the segment of the first conversation to them without merging the records. Asgar's voice cut off on the irritated confirmation: yes, he had seen it himself.

She returned to her station and opened the preparation for the next call. First they needed to find out where he was waiting and who was nearby. Below that, she left the question about the man at the door. Until the next connection, there was no one to ask it.
