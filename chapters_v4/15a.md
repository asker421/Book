Interlude III. Unfinished Lunch

She heard the click when he put the receiver down. The voice channel closed. In the neighboring field, the movement marker shifted away from the device.

"He stepped away," she said.

The engineer did not look up.

"You authorized entry?"

"After the check. He wants to say goodbye."

Now the engineer looked at her.

"For long?"

"I told him not to delay."

The neighboring panel continued updating. The external influence that had kept them from confirming transition stability had decreased. So far it had not returned, but the woman still watched the same area as though looking away would make her miss the beginning of a new rise.

A package of food lay on the table. Someone had put it beside her cup while she was talking to Asgar. One edge had already become wet. The woman moved the package aside and cleared space for her elbow.

The engineer returned to checking the external influence. The woman left that section to him and pulled the voice channel closer. Asgar had not returned; the food by her elbow was already in the way, but she did not get up to move it.

Last time he had asked where he would end up. She had answered immediately: the exit could not be determined. That had been followed by a silence she wanted to fill with something useful. Say that the state was better now, that the external influence had decreased, that they were continuing to work. She had remained silent. None of it answered his question.

The door behind her opened.

Korvin came in with Mirena. Mirena stopped beside the empty station without touching the controls and looked first at the channel, then at the cup and untouched food.

"What stage?"

"Outside. Entry permitted. Stepped away to say goodbye."

Mirena pulled a chair closer but did not sit yet.

Korvin remained behind them.

"You hand over after the door closes," he said.

"I can finish it."

"After the door closes."

The woman wanted to answer that it was not about fatigue. Then she saw how her fingers were gripping the edge of the table and released her hand.

"If the next exit is habitable, I have to tell him he can stay there."

"You will."

"And if he refuses because he still thinks we're taking him home?"

"Then first you tell him plainly: we're not taking him home. We don't know how."

She turned toward Korvin.

"And if after that he still wants to continue?"

"Then he decides."

"Even though the next transition could be worse?"

"It could."

"And we let him go?"

Korvin looked at her.

"Do you want to lock him in the first place where he can live?"

The woman looked at the empty channel.

"We know what he wants."

"Home. We don't know where, for him, the attempt to get home ends."

"Isn't that enough?"

"No."

She ran her thumb along the edge of the table.

"If a place appears where he can live and he goes farther only because he thinks we're still taking him home, I don't want to pretend that's a neutral choice."

"Then don't. Tell him he can stay. Tell him going on may be worse. Tell him we never promised him home."

"And after that let him go?"

"If he says 'I'm going'—yes."

"What if continuing is dangerous?"

"Then I won't allow it. That's why I'm here."

The woman exhaled briefly through her nose.

"You're defending his choice now?"

"No. I won't let you choose for him."

"Convenient wording."

Korvin looked at the engineer, then back at her.

"I've already seen an operator decide they knew what was better for a person. They pushed the procedure farther. We lost the person. Part of the circuit too."

For the first time in the conversation, she looked away.

"That wasn't here."

"Once was enough for me."

"I haven't violated a single authorization."

"Not yet."

"Then why are you taking me off?"

"Because you've already decided what his answer should be. And we don't even know where he'll come out yet."

She opened her mouth but said nothing.

Korvin nodded toward Mirena.

"Your shift is over."

"I'm not talking about fatigue."

"Neither am I. You've become attached to him. That's normal. At the console—it gets in the way."

"I'm not talking about the system."

"And I have to think about both it and him. That's why Mirena takes over."

The voice channel reappeared on the panel. Almost immediately, the door status changed.

All three fell silent.

The woman moved closer to the connection. Something knocked in the noise. Breathing could be heard—short, tense—then the sound became duller.

The door sealed.

She automatically reached to call Asgar, but the engineer raised a hand.

"Wait."

The transition measurements came unevenly. She saw the beginning of the familiar sequence, then several values disappeared. The neighboring field retained the previous data; their age increased.

"Is that current?" she asked.

"Not anymore."

She withdrew her hand.

The connection broke off in the middle of a faint rustle. The woman straightened and checked the audio level even though she could see the link was gone. Behind her, Korvin moved closer to the engineer.

The first updates returned separately.

The engineer waited for one more, enlarged the recording section.

"The door remains sealed. Continuing the check."

She looked at the empty channel field. Until there was a way to call Asgar, there was nothing to do there, but she still could not make herself move back.

Mirena sat beside her.

"Tell me what happened before this."

"The record is open."

"I'll read it. Tell me now."

The woman turned toward her, preparing to say that everything necessary had already been entered. Instead, she picked up the cup. A little cold liquid remained at the bottom; she did not want to drink it.

"He approached the telephone from outside. Reported that the external system called the state temporal anomaly. There was external influence on the boundary; I prohibited entry. After his access was checked, the influence decreased. Then he stepped away to say goodbye. I authorized entry only after reassessment."

Mirena listened without interrupting.

"What does he know about the next exit?"

"That we can't determine it."

"Did he ask again?"

"Yes."

The woman put down the cup. Its edge landed on the package and tipped; she had to catch it with both hands.

Mirena moved the food to a dry spot.

"How is he himself?"

"After the previous exit he was on his feet. He came over by himself just now and spoke coherently. He didn't report any injuries to me."

"Then I'll ask the rest."

She nodded. Suddenly she wanted to warn Mirena that Asgar did not like being spoken to as though all he had to do was comply. But after the conversation with Korvin, the thought sounded different. She did not know whether he liked any kind of conversation anymore, or whether she had the right to turn his irritation into a ready-made instruction for another person.

"When he asks where he'll end up," she said, "give him time after the answer."

Mirena looked at her.

"All right."

"And if there's an opportunity to stay, don't steer him toward an answer."

Mirena frowned slightly.

"You think I would?"

"I thought I wouldn't."

For several seconds they looked at each other.

"Understood," Mirena said.

Mirena opened the summary. Read the first lines, scrolled farther, then returned.

"It says here: 'External system classifies state as temporal anomaly.'"

"According to Asgar," the woman replied.

"Then I'll leave it that way."

She added the clarification and kept reading.

Korvin stepped away from the engineer.

"That's enough for you today."

The woman looked at him, then at the still-unavailable channel.

"I want to hear what he answers."

"They'll hear it here."

The woman pushed her chair back. Her legs had gone numb; she had to stand for a moment holding the backrest. Mirena took the vacated place and pulled the controls closer. The woman lingered on the cup: it was still where she had left it; a damp mark from her sleeve remained on the surface, and someone else was already preparing to work here.

The woman gathered the food package and cup.

At the door, she stopped.

"He may think something happened to me."

Mirena turned around.

"I'll tell him you were relieved."

"Tell him immediately."

"I will."

She went out into the corridor. It was cooler there. Voices came from the neighboring room; someone laughed quietly and immediately continued the conversation. The woman walked several steps, stopped by the wall, and finally opened the package.

The food had cooled and stuck to the tray. She separated a small piece but could not swallow it at once. She had to wash it down.

---

In the room, the engineer finished the check.

"The channel is available. We can call."

Mirena looked once more at the last conversation record. Then she moved it aside and started the call.

While the signal ran, she waited with her hand near the volume control.

The receiver was lifted at the other end.

"Asgar?" Mirena said.

"I'm here," he answered. "What happened?"
