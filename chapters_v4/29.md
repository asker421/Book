Chapter 29. Crumbs on the Table

A long ceiling light glowed beyond the glass. Beneath it stood a table with an open box, and the woman beside the table was staring into the cabin, still holding a piece of bread in her raised hand.

Asgar lifted the receiver.

"Mirena?"

The noise was faint and steady. He waited, then put the receiver down. The woman set the bread aside and stepped toward him. Seeing his hand on the door handle, she stopped, raised an open palm, and said something. Unfamiliar syllables came through the glass.

"I don't understand you."

She touched a device at the edge of the table. Her next sentence came from a small speaker by the door, delayed slightly behind the movement of her lips:

"Can you hear me now? Translation is active here. Don't open for a few more seconds: we're checking the air in the room and the pressure difference at your door."

"I hear you. Where am I?"

"At the receiving node. My name is Avelina. I'm responsible for the return of the device."

She looked at the work surface, then back at him.

"Pressure equalized. The air is suitable for you, temperature too. You can open."

Asgar opened the door a little, waited until it stopped resisting, and stepped out. Avelina moved back to clear his way. He set the bag beyond the threshold; for now, he kept the stick in his hand.

"Is this Earth?"

"No. An artificial station in the Solar System. We're orbiting the remnant of the Sun, beyond Earth's former orbit. The receiving equipment occupies the neighboring rooms. This is our workroom."

Behind her was a corridor with closed doors. The air smelled of bread. Asgar looked down: a seam between floor panels reached the red body and continued inside slightly farther to the left.

"What year is it now?"

"By which calendar?"

He looked up. Avelina saw his expression and took her hand away from the table.

"Sorry. You're asking in Earth terms. That calendar hasn't been used here for a long time. Settlements and systems keep their own counts; there is no single calendar 'now' for all of us."

"Did time end?"

"No. This conversation takes time. But I can't name one year for all these systems. If you need an interval from your date, it can be calculated in a chosen scale."

Asgar remembered asking Mirena to repeat the last number. Five billion and so many more digits that he had stopped being able to hold them together.

"Don't. I need August sixteenth, two thousand fourteen. Can you return me there?"

Avelina looked at him, then at the cabin. She did not answer for several seconds.

"You entered then?"

"Yes."

She pulled out a chair but did not sit. Asgar waited.

"We were preparing to receive the device," she said. "The operation contained an unresolved external load. From the old data, there was no way to know that a living person would arrive with it."

"I did. Can you do anything now?"

"I can check access to that event. First I need to read the record you arrived with."

Avelina turned the work surface toward him. Unfamiliar symbols changed to Russian labels.

"The text is translated too," she said. "Point it out if anything is unclear."

The top line read: RETRO-1.

Asgar leaned the stick against the wall and bent over the table.

"That's what it's called?"

"Yes. Designation of the first experimental device. Its own data matches the project data."

He read it again. Behind him stood the thing he had spent so long calling the booth. Someone had had a project for it.

"Did you build it?"

"No. We received the equipment and the obligation to complete the return. Before us, they were passed on to others. The people who assembled RETRO-1 lived long before this node."

"I was told: reach the source."

"You have. 'Source' is the old name for the receiving side. The place of assembly disappeared long ago, but the unfinished operation remained. What passed between nodes was not only archives: each successor received the previous node's receiving circuit itself and continued it. For RETRO-1, this is still the same side of the return. Now it is here."

Asgar turned toward the telephone.

"And Mirena? She spoke to me before the last transition."

Avelina opened another part of the record.

"Name her service if you know it."

"Emergency Support. Earth, two thousand seven hundred forty-four. She said she remained there when I transitioned. You have access to the device. Connect me to her."

Avelina read several lines, ran a finger along one of them, and stopped.

"The record of that connection is here. There is no active line."

"Where I was, they checked the sound. It worked."

"The local sound may have worked. The connection to their section of the device's history was what was breaking."

"Mirena explained how their channel worked. Why is it gone here?"

"Their connection held only across part of the return chain. Closer to the receiving side, the line ceased to exist. There is nothing left here to amplify."

Asgar touched the cradle hooks even though the receiver was resting correctly.

"And she won't know I made it?"

"Not through us. We have no other channel into her room."

He imagined Mirena sitting at her console. An engineer walks in, someone puts down a cup, the shift ends. He had never seen that room, only heard it: other voices, the movement of a chair, pauses in which she went away to verify an answer. All of it remained on Earth in her year.

"Did she at least know where I was going?"

"No. Before the transition, the service knew only what their part of the circuit provided and what you told them. The receiving side was not accessible to them."

Asgar moved away from the cabin. There was no one he could tell that he had found a person with the machine's project.

---

Avelina moved the box off the work surface. Crumbs remained where she had just opened the record; she had to brush them into her palm. Asgar pulled the chair closer and sat without taking off his jacket.

"Show me what's recorded about me."

Avelina displayed a short sequence of door states. Asgar recognized the open doorway, then the closed door. After it came the mass verification marker.

"I know that. I closed the door, then the extra mass was found. Is it still one record?"

"One. All the way to this room. Your exits from the cabin did not close it."

Asgar looked at the black receiver.

"Was the telephone part of the project too?"

"Yes. Voice service communication for maintenance."

"So even with the exact date, I was never in the assignment?"

"You weren't. RETRO-1 was supposed to travel empty."

"Then who chose the date?"

"The project builders. But they didn't pick it randomly from a calendar. That date was already in the historical materials."

Avelina opened a project page. At the top was 16.08.2014; below it, an image of a red object damaged so badly that fine details could not be made out.

Asgar moved closer. His son had turned ten. Katya had asked him to buy a large bottle of water and say when she should expect him because of the cake. All of that fit into eight digits on someone else's table.

"Where did they get this frame?"

"From historical materials. Only part of it reached the builders. The date, the place, the red form. From other appearances—measurements, geometry, traces of effects on the surroundings. They tried to understand what kind of device could have left them."

"And then they built their own."

"Gradually. They tested models, made prototypes. Some materials had to be invented. Some solutions were discarded for years. Here is the construction history."

She opened several records. Names stood beside diagrams; some assemblies were crossed out, others had rows of modifications. Asgar stopped her hand when she was about to scroll farther.

"But how did they know what to aim for?"

"They compared the result with what had survived of the red cabin. The more they managed to explain, the closer the construction became."

"To this cabin?"

"Yes."

He turned around. The door behind him had already settled back into the frame.

"And when they built it, they sent it to August sixteenth."

"It was the first full experiment."

Asgar looked again at the damaged frame.

"They built it because it had already existed."

Avelina did not hurry to close the page.

"That's why I don't have the name of the person who first invented it. Individual calculations have authors. There are people who assembled it by hand. But RETRO-1 as a device was reconstructed from its own trace."

"Even the booth?"

"History contained a red British telephone box. Their device received that profile and appeared where they knew it from."

"And the rest? Also from itself? I was shown the history of the research before. The physics developed separately from the red form."

"And that's true. They discovered the laws and methods themselves. But once they knew how to produce the required effects, the specific machine was checked against what had survived in history: geometry, modes, traces, sequence of events. The loop did not give them the physics from nothing. It specified what RETRO-1 itself had to become."

Asgar ran a finger along the edge of the table. He wanted to find a beginning: the person who had once decided to build this thing. At every step it was there again.

Avelina moved water toward him. He took the container but did not drink.

"Open that date for me."

---

She put a plan of the stadium corridor on the wall beside the cabin. The labels were in Russian. Asgar stood: the red rectangle, concrete wall, turn—it was recognizable even before Avelina showed the accessible section.

"This is the earliest confirmed node connected to your passenger record. We can begin the alignment from it. We combine this room with part of the space around that state of RETRO-1. You will step onto real concrete."

Asgar approached the plan.

"The whole stadium?"

"No. The corridor near the device and the section beyond the turn. By the calculation, the aligned geometry holds for roughly twelve meters from the body. Beyond that, the different data no longer assemble into a single space."

"And for another day?"

"Not on demand. First this node has to be passed. Then the system can check which other states can support a shared volume at all."

"I'm still outside then."

Avelina looked at the calculation result.

"Access begins before the door closes."

"So I can walk up to myself. Talk."

"You can."

He stood between the plan and the cabin. His phone would be there, Katya's voice. He could give his name. Say what would happen if he went inside.

"And stop him?"

"Try—yes. I don't know what will happen after intervention."

Asgar turned toward her.

"You don't know, or you don't allow it?"

"I don't know. I'm not going to decide what you tell him."

He looked at the short section beyond the turn.

"What if I go farther?"

Avelina looked at the diagram too.

"Beyond the reconstructed volume, I don't have aligned geometry. I can't tell you what will happen there because the system doesn't show it."

"The floor there is real."

"Up to the boundary—yes."

Asgar held his gaze on the end of the marked section.

"So you simply don't know."

"Yes. That's why I'm asking you not to test it on yourself."

"Will I have time before the door closes?"

"The phase includes the time before entry. It doesn't choose the exact action for you."

Asgar carried the bag to the table, took off his jacket, and placed it beside it. The stick remained by the wall. He pulled down the dark sleeve to cover the Caretaker's band and returned to the cabin.

Avelina showed him a light marker the receiving system had placed outside, beside the doorframe.

"It will go out when the door can be opened. Stay within the indicated section of the corridor. I'll be here at the controls."

"Open August sixteenth."

He pulled the handle, opened the door, and stepped inside. The telephone hung on the right in its familiar place. Beyond the glass, Avelina moved the chair away from the area beside the cabin and returned to the table. Asgar took the handle and closed the door.

The white wall beyond the glass began to dim.
