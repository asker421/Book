Chapter 29. Crumbs on the Table

A long ceiling light burned beyond the glass.

Beneath it stood a table with an open box.

A woman beside the table was staring into the booth, one hand still raised with a piece of bread in it.

Asgar lifted the receiver.

“Mirena?”

The line carried only a faint, steady hiss.

He waited.

Then put the receiver back.

The woman set down the bread and took a step toward him.

When she saw his hand on the door handle, she stopped, raised an open palm, and said something.

Unfamiliar syllables reached him through the glass.

“I don’t understand you.”

She touched a device at the edge of the table.

The next sentence came through a small speaker by the door, delayed slightly behind the movement of her lips.

“Can you understand me now?

“Translation is active here.

“Do not open the door for a few more seconds. We’re checking the room atmosphere and the pressure differential at your door.”

“I understand.

“Where am I?”

“At the receiving node.

“My name is Avelina.

“I’m responsible for completing the apparatus return.”

She glanced at the work surface, then back at him.

“Pressure equalized.

“The atmosphere is compatible with you.

“Temperature too.

“You can open the door.”

Asgar cracked the panel open, waited until the resistance eased, and stepped out.

Avelina moved back to give him room.

He set the bag just beyond the threshold.

Kept the walking stick in his hand for now.

“Is this Earth?”

“No.

“An artificial station in the Solar System.

“We orbit what remains of the Sun, beyond the former orbit of Earth.

“The receiving equipment occupies the adjacent rooms.

“This is our workroom.”

Behind her was a corridor lined with closed doors.

The air smelled of bread.

Asgar looked down.

A seam between floor panels ran toward the red shell and continued inside several centimeters to the left.

“What year is it?”

“According to which calendar?”

He looked up.

Avelina saw his expression and took her hand away from the table.

“Sorry.

“You mean Earth’s old calendar.

“We stopped using it a very long time ago.

“Settlements and systems maintain their own counts.

“There is no single calendar ‘now’ shared by everything here.”

“Did time end?”

“No.

“This conversation is taking time.

“But I cannot give you one year that applies to all of these systems.

“If you want the interval from your date, it can be calculated in a selected scale.”

Asgar remembered asking Mirena to repeat the previous number.

Five billion, followed by so many more places he had stopped being able to hold them together.

“Don’t.

“I need August sixteenth, two thousand fourteen.

“Can you send me back there?”

Avelina looked at him.

Then at the booth.

For several seconds she did not answer.

“You entered it on that date?”

“Yes.”

She pulled out a chair but did not sit.

Asgar waited.

“We prepared to receive the apparatus,” she said.

“The operation contained an unresolved external load.

“The old data did not tell us that a living person would reach the receiving side with it.”

“I did.

“So can you do something now?”

“I can check access to that event.

“First I need to read the record you arrived with.”

Avelina turned the work surface toward him.

The unfamiliar symbols became Russian labels.

“The text is translated too,” she said.

“Point out anything that doesn’t make sense.”

Across the top line appeared:

RETRO-1.

Asgar leaned the walking stick against the wall and bent toward the table.

“That’s what it’s called?”

“Yes.

“Designation of the first experimental apparatus.

“Its own data match the design record.”

He read it again.

Behind him stood the thing he had called the booth for so long.

Someone had once had a design for it.

“Did you build it?”

“No.

“We inherited the equipment and the obligation to complete the return.

“Before us, both had been passed through other receiving nodes.

“The people who assembled RETRO-1 lived long before this station existed.”

“I was told to reach the source.”

“You did.

‘Source’ is an old term for the receiving side.

“The physical place of assembly disappeared long ago.

“The unfinished operation remained.

“Between receiving nodes, more than archives were transferred:

each successor inherited the previous node’s receiving circuit and continued it.

“For RETRO-1, this is still the same return side.

“It is here now.”

Asgar turned toward the telephone.

“What about Mirena?

“She spoke to me until the last transition.”

Avelina opened another part of the record.

“Name her service if you know it.”

“Emergency support.

“Earth, 2744.

“She told me she stayed there when I transitioned.

“You have access to the apparatus.

“Connect me to her.”

Avelina read several lines.

Ran a finger along one.

Stopped.

“The record of that connection is here.

“There is no active line.”

“The audio worked where I was before.

“They checked it.”

“Local audio may have worked.

“The connection to their segment of the apparatus history was what was failing.”

“Mirena explained how their channel worked.

“Why doesn’t it exist here?”

“Their link survived only across part of the return chain.

“Closer to the receiving side, that line ceased to exist.

“There is nothing here to amplify.”

Asgar touched the cradle switch though the receiver sat correctly.

“So she never finds out I made it.”

“Not through us.

“We have no other channel into her room.”

He imagined Mirena sitting at her console.

An engineer walking in.

Someone placing a cup somewhere.

The shift ending.

He had never seen that room.

Only heard it:

voices in the background.

chairs moving.

pauses while she went to verify an answer.

All of it remained on Earth in her year.

“Did she at least know where I was going?”

“No.

“Before the transition, the service knew only what their segment of the circuit provided and whatever you told them.

“They had no access to the receiving side.”

Asgar stepped away from the booth.

There was no one left to tell Mirena that he had finally found someone with the machine’s design.

---

Avelina removed the box from the work surface.

Crumbs remained where she had just opened the record.

She brushed them into her palm.

Asgar pulled out a chair and sat without removing his jacket.

“Show me what you have recorded about me.”

Avelina opened a short sequence of door states.

Asgar recognized the open doorway.

Then the closed panel.

After that came the mass verification.

“I know this.

“I closed the door.

“Then it found the extra mass.

“It’s still one record?”

“One continuous record.

“Up to this room.

“Your exits from the booth did not close it.”

Asgar looked toward the black telephone.

“The telephone was part of the design too?”

“Yes.

“Voice service channel for maintenance.”

“And me?

“If they knew the date, I’m still not in the task?”

“No.

“There is no passenger in the task at all.”

“Then who chose the date?”

“The project builders.

“But they did not select it arbitrarily from a calendar.

“That date already existed in historical material.”

Avelina opened a design page.

At the top:

16.08.2014.

Below it, an image of a red object, degraded so badly that small details were unreadable.

Asgar moved closer.

His son had turned ten.

Katya had asked him to buy a large bottle of water and tell her when to expect him because of the cake.

All of that now fit inside eight digits on someone else’s table.

“Where did they get this image?”

“Historical material.

“Only part of it reached the builders.

“The date.

“The location.

“The red form.

“From later appearances, they also had measurements, geometry, and traces of interaction with the environment.

“They tried to determine what kind of apparatus could have produced them.”

“And then built their own.”

“Gradually.

“They tested models.

“Built prototypes.

“Some materials had to be created.

“Some solutions were discarded after years.

“This is the assembly history.”

She opened several records.

Names stood beside diagrams.

Some assemblies were crossed out.

Others showed long revision chains.

Asgar stopped her hand when she tried to scroll farther.

“But how did they know what they were working toward?”

“They compared each result with what had survived from the red booth.

“The more the design could explain, the closer they believed it was.”

“To this booth?”

“Yes.”

He turned.

The panel behind him had already returned to its frame.

“And when they finished it, they sent it to August sixteenth.”

“That was the first full experiment.”

Asgar looked again at the degraded frame.

“They built it because it had already existed.”

Avelina left the page open.

“That is why I do not have the name of the person who invented RETRO-1 first.

“Individual calculations have authors.

“There are people who physically assembled it.

“But RETRO-1 itself was reconstructed from its own trace.”

“Even the booth?”

“There was a red British telephone box in the historical material.

“Their apparatus took that external profile and appeared in the place from which they knew it.”

“And the rest?

“Also from itself?

“I was shown the history of the research before.

“The physics developed separately from the red shape.”

“That is also true.

“They discovered the laws and methods independently.

“But once they could produce the required effects, the specific machine was checked against what survived in history:

geometry.

operating modes.

traces.

event sequence.

“The loop did not give them the physics from nothing.

“It constrained what RETRO-1 had to become.”

Asgar ran a finger along the edge of the table.

He wanted to find the beginning.

The person who had once decided to build the thing.

Every step led back to the thing itself.

Avelina pushed water toward him.

He took the container but did not drink.

“Open that date for me.”

---

She projected the stadium service corridor onto the wall beside the booth.

The labels were Russian.

Asgar stood.

Red rectangle.

Concrete wall.

The turn.

He recognized everything before Avelina even showed him the available region.

“This is the earliest confirmed node connected to your passenger record.

“We can begin reconciliation here.

“We overlap this room with part of the space surrounding that state of RETRO-1.

“You will step onto real concrete.”

Asgar approached the plan.

“The whole stadium?”

“No.

“The corridor around the apparatus and the section beyond the turn.

“According to the calculation, mutually consistent geometry holds for roughly twelve meters from the shell.

“Beyond that, the different data no longer resolve into one space.”

“What about another day?”

“Not on demand.

“We have to pass through this node first.

“Afterward the system can test which other states can support shared volume at all.”

“I was still outside then.”

Avelina checked the result.

“Access begins before the door closes.”

“So I can approach myself.

“Talk.”

“You can.”

He stood between the plan and the booth.

His phone would be there.

Katya’s voice.

He could give his own name.

Say what would happen if he entered.

“And stop him?”

“You can try.

“I do not know what the intervention would do afterward.”

Asgar turned to her.

“You don’t know, or you won’t allow it?”

“I don’t know.

“I’m not going to decide what you tell him.”

He looked toward the short section beyond the turn.

“What if I keep walking?”

Avelina looked at the diagram too.

“Beyond the reconstructed volume, I have no consistent geometry.

“I cannot tell you what will happen there because the system does not produce an answer.”

“The floor is real.”

“Up to the boundary, yes.”

Asgar kept his eyes on the end of the marked section.

“So you simply don’t know.”

“Yes.

“That is why I’m asking you not to test it with your body.”

“Will I have time before the door closes?”

“The phase includes the interval before entry.

“It does not choose your exact action.”

Asgar carried the bag to the table, removed the jacket, and left it beside the bag.

The walking stick remained against the wall.

He pulled down the dark sleeve to cover the Keeper’s band and returned to the booth.

Avelina showed him a light marker the receiving system had projected outside beside the door frame.

“It goes dark when the door can be opened.

“Stay within the displayed region in the corridor.

“I’ll remain here at the controls.”

“Open August sixteenth.”

He pulled the handle.

The panel opened.

He stepped inside.

The telephone hung on the right in its familiar place.

Beyond the glass, Avelina moved a chair away from the platform by the booth and returned to the table.

Asgar took the handle and closed the door.

The white wall beyond the glass began to fade.
