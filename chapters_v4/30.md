Chapter 30. Within Arm's Reach

The stands exhaled in one long sound. Asgar recognized it before the corridor appeared beyond the glass: that was how the crowd reacted when the ball went wide of the goal. The white wall of the room was still showing faintly through the concrete. Then only the concrete remained—close, with a darkened strip near the floor.

In the side window stood a man with a mobile phone to his ear.

Asgar stepped back and hit his shoulder against the panel beside the telephone. The man outside tilted his head slightly, trying to hear the woman on the call. The familiar T-shirt stretched across his stomach. Asgar remembered putting it on before leaving, remembered planning to make it back for the cake. The face beyond the glass held none of the wariness or exhaustion he had grown used to in his own reflection. That man was examining the booth and listening to Katya.

The light marker by the frame went out. Asgar took the handle. The door opened and almost immediately struck the wall; warm air came through the gap. He put one shoulder through, turned sideways, and moved one leg over the threshold. The man with the mobile stepped back to make room. Asgar straightened in the corridor, still holding the edge of the door, then let it go.

Their eyes met.

He remembered that look from the inside. Remembered the man who had squeezed out through the narrow opening, stood for a moment as if gathering strength, then walked past. Now the door was slowly returning behind his back. The silent man stood where he had once stood, looking through his own eyes.

Asgar parted his lips. His own name was already ready to be spoken. He could touch the man's shoulder—it was right there—make him lower the phone from his ear and look more closely. Name Katya, the sons, something a stranger could not possibly know.

The man moved the mobile slightly away from his cheek.

Asgar drew breath. If he spoke now, the man would not go inside. Or he might still go inside, only later. And what would happen then to Asgar himself, to the whole journey, to the room where access back had finally been found? He knew only one sequence: the man left, the door opened, he went inside. That history had carried him alive to Avelina. He had no other one.

A single shout from the stands reached the corridor. Asgar turned away and walked past the man with the phone. His left foot came down more carefully than his right.

He reached the turn and stopped. Looked back at the red door. It had already closed; the man beside it also turned his head toward the cabin.

Asgar turned away from the booth and stepped around the corner.

"Are you all right?" someone called behind him.

Asgar raised a hand without turning around. His voice was already in his throat, but he forced himself to keep walking.

He could still turn back, call out, say at least: wait.

Asgar disappeared around the corner. A short empty corridor extended there. He walked several meters and stopped by the wall. Concrete crumbled beneath his fingers. On Avelina's plan, the shared section ended in this part of the corridor, but from here no boundary was visible: the same floor, the same walls. Ahead, the corridor ended at a blank wall. From around the turn came his own voice. Individual words were lost in the roar of the stadium, then one sentence came clearly:

"I'm going to step into the booth for a second. At least I'll be able to hear you in there."

Asgar took his hand off the wall. Took half a step back and stopped. The door creaked, metal touched concrete. He waited for footsteps coming toward him even though he knew what was happening at the door. He himself had squeezed through that gap holding the mobile in his right hand. He himself had reached for the handle from inside.

The door settled into the frame with a dull thud. For several more seconds Asgar stood motionless. There, beyond the wall, the man he had been that morning was already disappearing with the cabin. But this corridor remained. The stadium remained. Beyond its walls was the same August, the same city, and Katya had not yet lived a single day without him.

The thought came so simply that at first he did not understand why he had ever intended to return to Avelina. Katya was not in an inaccessible past. She was at home.

He pushed away from the wall and turned back. The service exit could be reached only through the turn where the booth had been. Beyond the turn was the concourse, then the street. He could take a car, call from the first phone he found, get there on his own. All of these were ordinary tasks, and their ordinariness made his chest tighten.

On the very first step, his right sole did not meet the concrete where it should have; the impact came a fraction of a second later.

Asgar stumbled, recovered, and ran toward the turn. His left leg carried the caution accumulated over two years of travel, but his body accelerated on its own. The turn did not come any closer. He could see his legs covering distance over the floor, hear his own footsteps, but the wall to his right remained beside him with the same crack. He ran a palm along it as he moved; the roughness stayed beneath his fingers for several seconds even though his hand should already have passed beyond it.

He stopped so sharply that his left foot rolled inward. The pain barely reached his awareness. Asgar looked at the turn he had been running toward. It was farther away than it could possibly be in this corridor.

"No."

He ran for the exit again. This time space answered immediately. The floor stretched beneath his feet, the corner ahead receded, and the roar of the stadium lengthened into a low continuous sound. Asgar slapped a palm against the wall, trying to catch hold of something that would stay still. The concrete beneath his fingers became smooth.

He jerked his hand away.

The stadium did not disappear at once. The gray wall remained in front of him, but the crack beneath his hand slid sideways as though the concrete had moved together with the corridor. A yellow line appeared straight through the floor. Where the turn had been, a transparent partition emerged, then a table, light, the familiar control room.

Asgar stood in the same place, only the corridor around him was gone.

For several seconds he could not reconcile that with his body. His right hand was still extended toward the place where the stadium wall had been. Under his feet was already the concrete of the technical hall.

Katya stood behind the partition.

The same light blouse. The same bag beside the table. Katya stood almost exactly where he had seen her before leaving.

"Katya!"

She saw him.

Asgar broke into a run. Katya turned toward the control-room door and pressed the device by the opening. The employee at the table raised his head; the door began to open.

Only several meters remained.

Asgar almost reached the pale line on the floor and crossed it with his right foot.

The support came late.

He swayed, recovered, and looked down. Katya was already at the glass with one palm pressed against it. Asgar raised his own toward hers.

"Katya!"

The speaker was silent.

Katya pulled the door toward herself.

Asgar stepped along the pale line, and the distance to her increased. He stopped—the hall seemed to release him, giving back several lost meters. He took another step, more slowly.

The floor stretched again.

Katya reached an arm through the opening door.

Asgar could see her fingers. They were very close and at the same time far too far away.

He moved faster.

The pale line split in two. His left foot landed somewhere other than where he had placed it. A smooth light floor from another room appeared through the concrete, then the edge of a table.

Asgar turned sideways, trying to go around it.

The table moved with him.

Katya struck the glass with her palm.

Asgar lunged toward her.

The distance broke completely. He fell forward with one arm outstretched, but beneath his palm the smooth surface of the Source room was already appearing. Katya remained in front of him for one more instant—behind the glass, beside the door, one arm extended.

"Katya!"

The white wall passed through her figure.

The hall vanished before his palm touched the floor.
---

The impact landed on the smooth surface of the Source room.

Asgar remained on all fours. For several seconds he stared at the place where Katya had just been, then snapped his head up.

Avelina stood by the table. She was looking not at the calculations but at him, and she did not move.

"Again."

She did not answer.

"Open it again."

Avelina looked at the work surface. Lines were rapidly going dark there.

"I can't."

Asgar stood up.

"She's there. She saw me. Open it."

"Asgar..."

"Open it."

Avelina shook her head.

"It's already too late. RETRO-1 completed the return."

He stared at her.

"What does completed mean?"

"While the return sequence was running, the Source held the shared volume. Now the circuit has closed. The device has already gone farther along its own loop."

"Bring it back."

"I can't."

Asgar looked at the place where the red cabin had stood.

It was gone.

Only an empty patch of floor remained beside the table.

"But I was there."

Avelina said nothing.

"There were only a few meters left."

He said it more quietly, as though the distance could still be preserved at least as a number.

Avelina lowered her eyes.

Asgar looked at the empty place again.

Avelina looked back at the work surface.

"Why didn't it leave me there?" Asgar asked.

She turned the record toward him. It showed the date: August twenty-fourth, two thousand thirty-nine.

"Why there?"

"I didn't choose it. After August sixteenth, the system checked the next confirmed states of RETRO-1. The states of August twentieth and August twenty-fourth, two thousand fourteen, didn't contain enough external geometry for a shared volume. The next suitable one was this hall."

"Because for this state of RETRO-1, you have already been preserved," Avelina said. "The you from then leaves 2039 with the device, and that same passenger record continues all the way here. The shared volume could briefly place you beside Katya. But leaving the present you there would give one state of RETRO-1 two different permanent continuations of the same load."

Asgar looked at the date.

"There were two of us at the stadium too."

"That didn't interfere. You were not becoming a second preserved RETRO-1 load there. You were simply present beside yourself inside the shared volume."

He turned away from the table.

Katya's hand appeared in front of his eyes again in the open doorway. Several meters. At the end—less.

"I could have stopped myself then."

Avelina did not answer.

"At the stadium. I could have spoken. Just said anything."

He remembered his younger self with the phone against his cheek. His name had already been in his mouth. Katya. The children. Any word would have been enough to make the history different.

"What would have happened?" Asgar asked.

"I don't know."

He closed his eyes.

Back then he had told himself he had no right to break the only sequence he knew. Now for the first time he saw the other part too: he had held on to it not only out of caution—as long as that sequence existed, it still contained a road to Katya. And it ended here, only a few meters away from her.

Asgar opened his eyes and looked at the empty patch of floor.

"Send me back to her."

Avelina shook her head.

"It's no longer possible. The return is complete. The shared circuit is closed. RETRO-1 has gone farther along its own loop."

"Completely?"

"Completely."

"And my record?"

Avelina looked at the work surface.

"It didn't close. RETRO-1 completed its return. Your passenger record remained open on the Source side."

Asgar looked at the empty place beside the table where the red cabin had just stood.
