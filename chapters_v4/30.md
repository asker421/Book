Chapter 30. Within Arm’s Reach

The stands exhaled in one long sound.

Asgar recognized it before the corridor appeared beyond the glass:

the sound a crowd made when the ball went wide.

The white wall of the room still showed faintly through the concrete.

Then only the concrete remained—

close, with a darkened strip near the floor.

A man stood beyond the side glass with a mobile phone to his ear.

Asgar stepped back and struck his shoulder against the panel beside the telephone.

The man outside tilted his head slightly, trying to hear the woman on the other end.

A familiar T-shirt stretched across his stomach.

Asgar remembered putting it on before leaving home.

Remembered planning to be back for the cake.

The face beyond the glass held none of the wariness or exhaustion he had grown used to seeing in his own reflection.

That man was studying the booth and listening to Katya.

The light marker beside the frame went dark.

Asgar took the handle.

The door opened and almost immediately struck the wall.

Warm air came through the narrow gap.

He turned one shoulder, squeezed sideways, and put one leg across the threshold.

The man with the phone stepped back to give him room.

Asgar straightened in the corridor, still holding the edge of the panel.

Then let go.

Their eyes met.

He remembered that look from the other side.

Remembered the man who had squeezed out of the narrow opening, stood as though gathering strength, then walked past him.

Now the panel was slowly swinging closed behind Asgar.

The silent man stood where Asgar himself had stood.

Looking through his eyes.

Asgar parted his lips.

His own name was already there, ready to be spoken.

He could touch the man’s shoulder—

it was right there—

make him move the phone away from his ear.

Make him look properly.

Say Katya’s name.

His sons’ names.

Something no stranger could know.

The man lowered the mobile slightly from his cheek.

Asgar drew breath.

If he spoke now, the man would not enter.

Or perhaps he still would.

Only later.

And then what happened to Asgar?

To the whole journey?

To the room where access to the past had finally been found?

He knew only one sequence:

the man left.

the door opened.

Asgar entered.

That sequence had carried him alive all the way to Avelina.

He had no second history to compare it with.

A single shout reached the corridor from the stands.

Asgar looked away and stepped past the man with the phone.

His left foot landed more carefully than the right.

He could still turn.

Call out.

Say only:

wait.

He reached the turn and stopped.

Looked back at the red door.

It had already closed.

The man beside it had turned his head toward the booth too.

Asgar went around the corner.

A short empty corridor extended beyond it.

He walked several meters and stopped beside the wall.

Concrete crumbled beneath his fingertips.

On Avelina’s plan, the shared region ended somewhere farther ahead.

From here there was no visible boundary.

Same floor.

Same walls.

Occasional service doors beneath utility signs.

Beyond the turn, he could hear his own voice.

Most words vanished beneath the stadium noise.

Then one sentence came through clearly.

“I’ll step into the booth for a second. We’ll finish talking, then I’ll go.”

Asgar took his hand from the wall.

Moved half a step back.

Stopped.

The door creaked.

Metal touched concrete.

He waited for footsteps coming toward him even though he knew what was happening at the door.

He himself was squeezing through that gap, holding the mobile in his right hand.

He himself was looking for the inner handle.

The lock clicked.

Asgar stood motionless for several more seconds.

Behind the wall, the man he had been that morning was already disappearing with the booth.

But the corridor remained.

The stadium remained.

Beyond those walls was the same August.

The same city.

Katya had not yet lived even one day without him.

The thought came so simply that at first he did not understand why he had ever intended to return to Avelina.

Katya was not in an inaccessible past.

She was at home.

He pushed away from the wall and headed toward the service exit.

After several steps he was running.

His left leg still carried the caution accumulated across two years of the journey, but his body accelerated on its own.

A green exit sign glowed above the door ahead.

Beyond it should be stairs.

Then the street.

After that he could take a car.

Use the first phone he found.

Get there himself.

All of those were ordinary problems.

Their ordinariness made his chest tighten.

Around the place where Avelina’s plan showed the calculated region ending, his right sole did not meet concrete where the floor should have been.

The impact came a fraction of a second later.

Asgar stumbled, recovered, and kept running.

The exit sign did not come closer.

He could see his legs covering distance.

Hear his own footsteps.

But the wall to his right remained beside him with the same crack.

Asgar dragged his palm across it as he ran.

The rough surface stayed beneath his fingers for several seconds longer than it should have.

He stopped so abruptly that his left ankle rolled inward.

The pain barely reached awareness.

Asgar looked back toward the turn he had come from.

It was farther away than the corridor could possibly contain.

“No.”

He ran toward the exit again.

This time space responded immediately.

The floor stretched beneath him.

The green sign became thin and distant.

The stadium roar drew out into one low continuous tone.

Asgar struck the wall with his palm, trying to find anything that would stay still.

The concrete beneath his fingers became smooth.

He pulled his hand away.

The stadium did not vanish all at once.

The gray wall remained in front of him, but the crack beneath his palm slid sideways as though the corridor itself had moved.

A yellow line appeared through the floor.

Where the stair door had been, a transparent partition emerged.

Then a table.

Light.

The familiar control room.

Asgar stood in the same place.

Only the corridor was gone.

For several seconds his body could not reconcile it.

His right arm was still extended toward the wall that had existed in the stadium.

Beneath his feet was now the concrete floor of the technical hall.

Katya stood behind the partition.

Same pale blouse.

Same bag beside the table.

For her, less than a minute had passed since he left.

“Katya!”

She saw him.

Asgar ran.

Katya turned toward the control-room door and pressed the device beside the opening.

The employee at the desk looked up.

The panel began sliding open.

Only several meters remained.

Asgar nearly reached the pale line on the floor and crossed it with his right foot.

The support came late.

He swayed, recovered, looked down.

Katya was already at the glass with one palm pressed against it.

Asgar raised his own toward hers.

“Kat!”

The speaker was silent.

Katya pulled the door toward herself.

Asgar stepped along the pale line.

The distance to her increased.

He stopped.

The hall seemed to release him and return several lost meters.

He took another step, slower.

The floor stretched again.

Katya reached one arm through the opening.

Asgar could see her fingers.

Only a little distance remained.

And at the same time far too much.

He moved faster.

The pale line split in two.

His left foot landed to the side of where he had placed it.

Smooth light flooring from another room appeared through the concrete.

Then the edge of a table.

Asgar turned sideways, trying to go around it.

The table moved with him.

Katya struck the glass with her palm.

Asgar lunged toward her.

Distance broke completely.

He fell forward with one arm extended, but beneath his hand the smooth surface of the source room was already appearing.

Katya remained in front of him for one more instant—

behind the glass.

by the door.

arm outstretched.

“Katya!”

A white wall passed through her figure.

The hall disappeared before his hand touched the floor.

---

The impact came against the smooth surface of the source room.

Asgar remained on hands and knees.

For several seconds he stared at the place where Katya had just been.

Then snapped his head up.

Avelina stood beside the table.

She was not looking at the calculations.

She was looking at him.

Something in her face had changed.

That made it worse.

“Again.”

She did not answer.

“Open it again.”

Avelina looked at the work surface.

Lines were fading rapidly.

“I can’t.”

Asgar stood.

“She’s there.

“She saw me.

“Open it.”

“Asgar…”

“Open it.”

Avelina shook her head.

“It’s too late.

“RETRO-1 completed the return.”

He stared at her.

“What do you mean, completed?”

“While the return sequence was active, the source maintained shared volume.

“Now the circuit has closed.

“The apparatus has already continued along its own loop.”

“Bring it back.”

“I can’t.”

Asgar looked toward the place where the red booth had stood.

It was gone.

Only an empty section of floor remained beside the table.

“But I was there.”

Avelina said nothing.

“I was only a few meters from her.”

He said it more quietly, as though the distance could still be preserved if he kept it as a number.

Avelina lowered her eyes.

Asgar looked again at the empty space.

Only seconds ago, Katya had been on the other side.
