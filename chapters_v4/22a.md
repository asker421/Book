Interlude IV. In the Margins of the Record

The engineer turned his panel toward Mirena.

A long strip ran across it—the readings from every instrument in the booth during the minutes when Asgar had closed the door for the first time.

The engineer had pulled up that interval to answer Asgar’s question about the man in the corridor, and he had not moved away from it since.

Closer to the beginning were several measurements that had never received an explanation during the original review.

Mirena had not yet cleared the finished conversation from her own field.

Asgar had received his answer and hung up.

There was no reason to call him again.

“Here,” the engineer said.

“Before the door closed.”

Mirena found the faint external deviation.

Beside it was an old note:

*Physical interpretation not established.*

She opened the attached voice record.

Asgar was describing the man who had come out before him.

The woman on the line was asking him to confirm what he had seen.

Mirena stopped playback when the engineer turned his result toward her.

“Look at this first without the conversation.”

On his panel, the early marker sat among several others.

Previously they had fallen into different groups.

Now, processing against the source model was clustering some of them together.

Above the group was a label:

*Associated with return history.*

Mirena selected the early signal and opened the old processing.

Same weak sequence.

It had not become clearer.

But now it could be linked to other events that had previously been treated separately.

“What connects them?”

The engineer opened the explanation.

Mirena began reading, then asked him to expose the basis of one transition.

He did.

Then another.

The deeper they went, the less the label resembled an answer.

The classifier was using a network of dependencies the engineer could not yet translate into a clear physical statement.

“What happens if you remove the first trace?” Mirena asked.

He looked at her.

“From the test.

“So we don’t make the others fit it.”

The engineer excluded the early interval and ran the processing again.

Mirena returned to her station to check the module state.

No new call had come in.

She removed the finished conversation from the upper field, clearing it for the next data, and returned to the engineer before the result arrived.

The cluster remained.

When he restored the early record, it again fell among the similar events.

Mirena placed the old and new results side by side.

Before this check, the early trace could be set aside while they focused on what had begun after the door closed.

Now they had to bring it back into the same analysis.

She pushed the old note toward the engineer.

“This needs an addition.”

“I still haven’t established the cause.”

“That’s what the note says.

“But now it isn’t isolated.”

The engineer changed the tolerances.

Some markers dropped out of the group.

Several remained.

Mirena watched the early one.

Under this check, it held.

The engineer saved both variants and began preparing the material for independent processing.

The package included the source sequences, the earlier result, and the new model.

Asgar’s account remained a separate attachment.

“If the independent check confirms the cluster, what do we tell him?” Mirena asked.

“That we found similar registrations.

“I still can’t tell him anything about the man from them.”

She opened the record of the previous conversation and found the part where Asgar had spoken about her shift.

She listened to those few seconds twice.

“He was wrong,” the engineer said without turning.

“He was.”

“But you’re listening.”

“I’m checking whether I answered him worse than I remember.”

Mirena closed the recording.

She had answered exactly as she remembered.

That did not make it easier.

She asked the engineer to send the result to her too.

He added her to the recipients, then returned to the beginning of the sequence.

The marker for the first door closure moved beyond the right edge of the enlarged interval.

Mirena stayed beside him.

Before, she had opened this section to hear the voice of a man who still did not know what had happened to him.

Now the engineer was examining what had been recorded before him.

She returned to the line, leaving the early interval open on the neighboring panel.
