Interlude II. Beyond Authorization

The engineer removed the weight of the living person from his calculation and started it again. At her own console, the woman was still closing the record of the conversation: Asgar had said that people were staying outside and had entered. The final confirmation of the door arrived already amid interference. She left it on her panel and turned toward the neighboring desk.

Several branches disappeared from the second calculation. Sections that had previously broken into broad bands became narrower. The woman pulled her chair closer: for the first time in this work, the difference was visible without magnification.

"Is he interfering here?"

"His contribution changes the stability. And the shape of these sections. Look farther on."

She followed the sequence to the end, then returned to the original version. The overall direction remained. The engineer was already restoring the excluded contribution piece by piece. The woman waited with her palm near the edge of the panel so he would not close the comparison yet. The lines diverged again in individual sections and then continued in the same direction as before.

"Did you leave the direction as a condition?"

"No. I checked that first too."

He opened the input data. She went through them and asked him to restore two values he had left within the margin of error. The new calculation took longer. The engineer opened the previous results on the neighboring panel. By the time he found the relevant section for comparison, the sequence had finished building.

The woman switched between the variants herself. With Asgar's contribution. Without it. The absence of living mass removed some of the complications, but the movement in the calculation continued.

"So his presence isn't enough," she said. "We've been looking only at what he changed."

"Now we'll have to look at what remains without him too."

She moved her hand away. The confirmation of the closed door was still on her panel. Asgar was inside, continuing on, and for the first time they had managed to remove him from the model without stopping it.

"Can we separate him?"

The engineer returned to the current-state data. He checked in silence, and she did not hurry him, even though the answer she needed might amount to a single word.

"On the panel—yes. With the person, no. We don't have a safe method."

"He found his wife."

The engineer stopped scrolling.

"Is she nearby?"

"He only said she's here."

He looked at his calculation, then at her panel.

"We spent half the day taking him out of the model," he said. "By evening I stopped thinking about the fact that I was taking out a person."

"I know."

"That wasn't a reproach."

"I didn't take it as one."

She opened the segment of the conversation. Asgar was saying that his wife was here, that he wanted to stay. The woman stopped playback before her own answer began. She remembered explaining that the connection would remain, how he had asked about the concrete that had chipped near the body. Then he had gone to speak with his wife and returned.

"Can we still get that state?" she asked. "If he asks to go back."

The engineer opened the information for the completed connection. She recognized the check he had already performed before Asgar entered: permissibility of holding, risk of increasing the discrepancy, no confirmed method of extending access. Now the connection had ended.

"The record remains. We don't know how to restore that state from it."

The woman closed the conversation. Removing the mass on the panel had taken one action. To Asgar, she would again have to say there was no available method.

"Send the full comparison to the next group. Have them check the direction separately. If you leave only the improvement in stability, they'll start again with how to remove him from the calculation."

The engineer saved the variants together. As he was sending them, he stopped and disabled the model overlay on the source measurements. A faint trace remained on a separate row.

The woman recognized the position of the markers but asked him to open the first case beside it. The new trace resembled the early deviation before Asgar's registration. They did not match completely even after the scale was aligned.

"Did we send anything then?" she asked.

"There are no commands in this interval."

He checked the movements of the living mass and found no match there either. The woman looked at the two traces. She wanted to bring back the record about the man and place it beside them, but the missing measurement could not be recovered from a voice.

"Save it separately. Don't include it in the explanation of the direction yet."

The engineer left the trace for further analysis and sent the comparison of the calculations. An option to check the voice channel appeared on her panel. The woman pulled the microphone closer and opened the information on Asgar's state after the transition. If he asked to return to his wife, she would be the one who had to answer.
