Interlude II. Beyond Tolerance

The engineer removed the weight of a living person from his calculation and started the count again. At her own console, the woman was still closing the conversation record: Asgar had said that the people were staying outside and had entered. The final door confirmation arrived already amid interference. She left it on her panel and turned toward the neighboring desk.

Several branches disappeared from the second calculation. Sections that had previously broken apart into broad bands became narrower. The woman pulled her chair closer: for the first time in this work, the difference was visible without magnification.

"Is this where he's interfering?"

"His contribution changes the stability. And the shape of these sections. Look farther."

She followed the sequence to the end, then returned to the original version. The overall direction remained. The engineer was already restoring the excluded contribution piece by piece. The woman waited with one hand at the edge of the panel so he would not close the comparison yet. The lines separated again in individual sections and then continued in the same direction as before.

"Did you leave the direction as a condition?"

"No. I checked that first too."

He opened the input data. She went through it and asked him to restore two values he had left within the error margin. The new calculation took longer. The engineer opened the previous results on the adjacent panel. By the time he found the necessary section for comparison, the sequence had finished building.

The woman switched between the variants herself. With Asgar's contribution. Without it. Removing the living mass eliminated some of the complications, but the movement in the calculation continued.

"So his appearance isn't enough," she said. "We've been looking only at what he changed."

"Now we'll have to look for what remains without him too."

She moved her hand away. The confirmation of the closed door was still displayed on her panel. Asgar was inside, continuing the journey, and here for the first time they had managed to remove him from the model and still not stop it.

"Can he be separated?"

The engineer returned to the current-state data. He checked in silence, and she did not rush him even though the answer she needed might have been only one word.

"On the panel—yes. With a person, no. We don't have a safe way."

"He found his wife."

The engineer stopped scrolling.

"She's nearby?"

"He only said she's here."

He looked at his calculation, then at her panel.

"We spent half the day removing him from the model," he said. "By evening I stopped thinking of it as removing a person."

"I know."

"That wasn't a reproach."

"I didn't take it as one."

She opened the segment of the conversation. Asgar was saying that his wife was here, that he wanted to stay. The woman stopped playback before her own answer began. She remembered explaining that the connection would remain, how he had asked about the concrete that had chipped near the body. Then he had gone to speak to his wife and returned.

"Can that state still be obtained?" she asked. "If he asks to go back."

The engineer opened the details of the completed connection. She recognized the check he had already run before Asgar entered: permissibility of maintaining it, the risk of increasing the discrepancy, the lack of a confirmed way to prolong access. Now the connection had ended.

"The record remains. We don't know how to restore that state from it."

The woman closed the conversation. Removing the mass from the panel had taken a single action. To Asgar, she would again have to say there was no available method.

"Send the entire comparison to the next group. Have them check the direction separately. If you leave only the improvement in stability, they'll start again from how to remove him from the calculation."

The engineer saved the variants together. As he was sending them, he stopped and disabled the model overlay on the raw measurements. A faint trace remained on a separate row.

The woman recognized the placement of the markers but asked him to open the first case alongside it. The new trace resembled the early deviation before Asgar's registration. Even after aligning the scale, there was no full match.

"Did we send anything then?" she asked.

"There are no commands in that interval."

He checked the movements of living mass and found no match there either. The woman looked at the two traces. She wanted to bring back the record of the man and place it beside them, but the missing measurement could not be extracted from a voice recording.

"Save it separately. Don't include it in the direction explanation yet."

The engineer left the trace for further analysis and sent the comparison of the calculations. On her panel, the option to check the voice channel appeared. The woman pulled the microphone closer and opened the data on Asgar's condition after the transition. If he asked about returning to his wife, she would be the one who had to answer.
