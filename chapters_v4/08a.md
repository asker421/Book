Interlude II. Beyond Tolerance

The engineer removed the weight of a living person from his calculation and ran it again from the beginning.

At her own console, the woman was still closing the record of the conversation: Asgar had said that people would remain outside, then entered. The final confirmation that the door had closed arrived already buried in interference.

She left it on her panel and turned toward the next desk.

Several branches disappeared from the second calculation. Sections that had previously spread into broad bands became narrower.

The woman pulled her chair closer. For the first time during this work, the difference was visible without magnification.

“This is where he interferes?”

“His contribution changes the stability. And the shape of these sections. Look farther on.”

She followed the sequence to the end, then returned to the original version.

The overall direction remained.

The engineer was already restoring the excluded contribution piece by piece. The woman waited with one hand near the edge of the panel so he would not close the comparison yet.

The lines separated again in several sections, then continued in the same direction as before.

“Did you keep the direction as a condition?”

“No. That was the first thing I checked too.”

He opened the input data.

She went through it, then asked him to restore two values he had left within the margin of error.

The new calculation took longer.

The engineer opened the previous results on the adjacent panel. By the time he found the relevant section for comparison, the new sequence had finished building.

The woman switched between the versions herself.

With Asgar’s contribution.

Without it.

Removing the living mass eliminated some of the complications, but the movement in the model continued.

“So his appearance isn’t enough,” she said. “We’ve been looking only for what he changed.”

“Now we’ll have to look at what remains without him too.”

She took her hand away.

The confirmation of the closed door was still on her panel.

Asgar was inside, continuing along the route, while here for the first time they had managed to remove him from the model without stopping it.

“Can we separate him?”

The engineer returned to the data for the current state.

He checked in silence, and she did not hurry him even though the answer she needed might be a single word.

“On the panel, yes. Not with the person. We don’t have a safe way.”

“He found his wife.”

The engineer stopped scrolling.

“She’s outside?”

“In the same hall. Behind a partition.”

He looked at his calculation, then at her panel.

“We spent half the day removing him from the model,” he said. “By evening I stopped thinking of what I was removing as a person.”

“I know.”

“That wasn’t an accusation.”

“I didn’t take it as one.”

She opened the relevant part of the conversation.

Asgar was saying that his wife was there, that he wanted to stay.

The woman stopped playback before her own answer began.

She remembered explaining that the connection would remain, remembered him asking about the concrete that had broken away beside the structure.

Then he had gone back to his wife and returned.

“Can that state still be recovered?” she asked. “If he asks to go back.”

The engineer opened the data for the completed connection.

She recognized the checks he had already run before Asgar entered: whether the state could be held, the risk of increasing the discrepancy, the absence of any confirmed way to extend access.

Now the connection was over.

“The record remains. We don’t know how to restore that state from it.”

The woman closed the conversation.

Removing the mass from a panel had taken one action.

To Asgar, she would once again have to say that no available method existed.

“Send the full comparison to the next team. Have them check the direction separately. If they only see the improved stability, they’ll start again with the question of how to remove him from the calculation.”

The engineer saved the versions together.

Just before sending them, he stopped and disabled the model overlay on the source measurements.

A faint trace remained on an isolated stream.

The woman recognized the placement of the markers but asked him to bring up the first event beside it.

The new trace resembled the early deviation from before Asgar’s registration.

Even after aligning the scale, it was not a complete match.

“Did we send any command then?” she asked.

“There are no commands in this interval.”

He checked the movement of the living mass and found no correspondence there either.

The woman looked at the two traces.

She wanted to bring back the record about the man and place it beside them, but a voice recording could not supply the missing measurement.

“Save it separately. Don’t include it in the explanation of the direction yet.”

The engineer set the trace aside for further analysis and sent the calculation comparison.

On her panel, the option to test the voice channel appeared.

The woman pulled the microphone closer and opened the status data for Asgar after the transition.

If he asked to return to his wife, she would be the one who had to answer.
