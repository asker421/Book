Chapter 12. A Crack in the Order

They went out together when the car arrived.

Asgar locked the room and automatically checked the door with his palm. Martin was already waiting at the turn. He had a screen under his arm and the translator on his chest; he had taken nothing else.

The passage smelled of damp flooring. Somewhere the floor had just been washed. Asgar stepped over a dark strip by the wall and for a second thought he would have to walk around this place again in the evening.

The car was waiting by the service exit.

On the way, Martin read through Inessa's messages. Asgar looked outside. The buildings that had become familiar over the last few days quickly fell behind; beyond them came long structures separated by drives and strips of greenery. Above one intersection, a construction turned slowly, casting a broad shadow across the road.

"Is it still there?" Asgar asked.

Martin turned the screen toward him.

In the image, the booth stood beside closed freight gates. A pale line ran along the floor. One of its branches reached the red body and ended there.

"Is this now?"

"With a delay of a few seconds."

Asgar leaned closer.

He wanted to make out the handle. Understand whether the door would open, from which side to approach it. No one moved in the recording.

"Did they get the people out?"

"Out of the passage—yes. They're checking the other exits."

Martin put the screen away when the car turned.

Barriers stood ahead. Several service vehicles were beyond them. A person at the entrance raised a hand, waited for them to stop, and looked inside. Martin showed him the message. They were let through, but from there they had to continue on foot.

Inessa met them near a side entrance.

"The cabin is two turns from here," the device on her chest translated. "For now we stay here. The main passage is clear, but not all automatic doors have been disabled."

Asgar looked at the door.

"They can close?"

"They can. Until we check them all, we don't go inside."

She turned to an approaching employee. They spoke quickly; Asgar caught a familiar word meaning exit, then lost the thread. Martin's translator relayed the end:

"...not according to the diagram. Have the person walk all the way to the stairs."

Inessa waited for confirmation and led them inside.

---

The observation room was cramped with people.

A table with several screens stood against the wall. One showed the booth from the front, another from the side and above. A third displayed a plan of the complex. Colored regions on it changed, and Asgar did not understand what they represented.

He stopped behind Martin.

In the side view, the booth almost touched the gates. In the front view, there was enough space between them for a person to pass.

Asgar pointed at the screens.

"Are these different times?"

The employee at the table looked at him, then at Martin. Martin repeated the question. The man checked the markers.

"One interval," came the translation.

Asgar looked at both screens again. In one, a passage remained between the booth and the gates. In the other, almost none. The cameras showed the same time—and different space.

Inessa moved closer.

"Keep both images separate. Do not update the combined model."

Part of the markings on the plan disappeared. The employee explained something, running a finger over two areas. Inessa interrupted him; the translation lagged and overlapped the beginning of the next sentence.

Martin touched the device on his chest.

"Wait. I'll leave only her channel."

The speaker relayed:

"I don't need the most likely route. I need to know where the people are. Confirm by voice."

If two systems could not even agree on where open space was, a safe route on the screen guaranteed nothing.

Asgar stepped away from the table to make room.

A low rumble rolled through the corridor beyond the door.

One of the employees looked up. In the frontal image, a broad gray shutter above the booth began to descend. It moved slowly, closing the upper part of the passage.

Inessa was already speaking into comms.

"Stop the drive. Local shutdown."

On the screen, the shutter jerked.

Asgar heard a metallic impact—first from the corridor, then, delayed, from the speaker. The lower edge of the partition went crooked. A mounting tore free from the side guide and struck the floor.

Someone at the table swore.

The shutter stopped, leaving the passage open beneath it. Dust hung nearby.

Inessa looked at the plan.

"Everyone out of the inner zone. Via the side stairs. Confirm exits by people, not by counter."

Martin took Asgar by the forearm and led him toward the door.

They went out into the corridor. Two employees with portable screens followed them. One tried to go back, but Inessa stopped him with a gesture.

"Recording stays. People leave."

Asgar understood her before the translation.

---

The cold outside immediately got under his T-shirt.

They stopped behind a barrier by the service drive. People continued to emerge from the side door. Inessa stood where she could see each of them and checked something on her device.

Asgar counted with her even though he did not know how many were supposed to come out.

When the last one emerged, she put the device away and looked at Asgar.

"You asked what another week would change."

He did not answer.

Inessa turned toward the door no one else was coming through and gave someone the number of people who had exited.

Asgar looked at the door.

"Why did it come down?"

Martin listened to messages reaching Inessa, then removed the translator from his clothing and set it on the high concrete ledge between them.

"The safety system decided the hazardous zone had expanded and tried to cut it off with the shutter."

"And the booth beneath it?"

"Another system showed an open passage there."

"So one system tried to close an area another thought was clear?"

"Yes."

Asgar remembered the two images of the gates.

Inessa came over and heard the end of the translation.

"The drive didn't receive a central command. It was on local backup. It's been physically opened now, and they're securing the shutter from outside the zone."

"Will you shut everything down?"

"Everything that can move, close, or turn on its own. We'll leave ventilation and fire protection, but separate them from the rest of the control system."

She looked at Martin.

"No research today."

He nodded but immediately turned his screen toward her.

"We can leave the passive channels. On different systems the clocks—"

"Leave the recording. Don't start anything to clarify it."

Martin lowered the screen.

Asgar heard metal strike again behind the wall. Inessa raised a hand and listened to a message.

"They're securing the shutter," she said. "Don't go back."

Asgar looked down: during the conversation, he had moved one step closer to the building.

---

While they waited, Asgar received a call on the work tablet.

A message from the phone specialist appeared on the screen: the inspection time was confirmed, the device could be brought in after lunch.

Asgar read it and closed it.

Martin stood beside him but asked nothing.

The last employee from the internal team came out of the complex. There was white dust on his sleeve. Inessa stopped him, listened, then called someone over comms.

The image of the passage appeared again on the portable screen.

The shutter no longer moved. The torn mounting lay by the wall. The booth stood beneath the skewed edge without touching it.

Asgar leaned closer.

"That line on the right was whole."

He pointed at the seam between the floor panels.

Now the image showed the seam splitting into two branches. Between them lay a narrow wedge of surface, as though part of the floor had been rotated by a small angle.

Inessa enlarged the area.

The image trembled and returned. The wedge remained.

"Is that after shutdown?" Martin asked.

The employee at the screen checked the marker and confirmed.

Asgar straightened.

"Then it isn't the automation."

Inessa requested the second camera. From the other angle, a thin crack was visible at the same place. Its edge was still pale from the fresh break.

She ordered the perimeter widened.

Behind them, vehicles began to move away.

Asgar watched a driver take a cup off the roof, get inside, and reverse. People moved calmly, without running, but the drive became emptier and emptier.

"Is the phone ringing?" he asked.

Martin looked at the employee.

The man turned on audio from the camera.

Through the ventilation noise came the familiar ring.

Asgar exhaled and rubbed his face with both hands.

"I want to talk to her."

---

Inessa first called the people checking the side passage and asked them to show the route with a handheld camera. Asgar stood beside her and watched: a door, short corridor, turn, open area in front of the booth. There was no need to walk beneath the skewed shutter. The device could be approached from the side while staying on the intact part of the floor.

"If anything changes under your feet, you go back. No 'one more step,'" Inessa said.

Asgar nodded.

Martin took his tablet and checked the connection.

"Keep translation on. If Inessa speaks, you'll hear her."

"I'll have the receiver there."

"Only while you're walking. Put it away at the door."

Asgar looked at the thin device.

"I'd rather call from here before I approach. I don't want to leave anything near it."

Inessa heard and nodded.

"Fine. If the connection drops before the open area, you turn around. After that we watch through the camera. Anything changes—you move away immediately, don't wait for instructions."

Then he walked toward the side entrance.

Inside, it smelled of dust and heated metal. His own footsteps were too distinct. At the turn, he stopped and looked back. Martin was visible in the open doorway. He did not go any farther.

Asgar raised a hand and continued.

The booth came fully into view.

The shutter hung farther left, in the main passage. A bent rod protruded from the torn mounting. Asgar tried not to look beneath it, but his gaze kept returning.

Only a few steps of flat floor remained to the red door.

He reported that he had arrived, put away the tablet, and pulled the handle toward himself.

The door opened.

Asgar lifted the receiver without crossing the threshold.

"I'm here."

"Asgar. I hear you."

"Nineteen days."

The woman asked what he meant.

"I've lived here nineteen days. Now it appeared in another place. They got the people out, but the building is breaking."

He told her about the shutter, the crack, the disabled drives. The woman asked him to clarify whether the changes continued after shutdown.

"Yes. I saw it myself on two cameras."

An indistinct voice sounded on the line. She answered someone, but Asgar could not make out the words.

"Yes. We're seeing the same thing here," she finally said.

"Can you remove the cabin?"

"Not right now."

"Do I have to go in?"

"After your previous entries, the changes stopped. We don't know why. I won't tell you that going in will definitely help. It won't repair the damage in any case."

Asgar looked at the door. The same scratch was still there beside the handle.

"What if I don't go in?"

"You can wait. The cabin may leave on its own. But I don't know what will change before that happens."

"So we can just wait."

"You can. If the people are already clear."

He listened to her breathing between the words.

"You still haven't reached the source?"

"No. We don't have a connection to it yet."

Asgar closed his eyes.

Something small clinked behind him.

He turned immediately. Dust was falling from the ceiling near the far wall. The red body remained still.

"I need to move away," he said.

"Put the receiver down. Don't pull the cord."

He put it on the cradle and stepped back from the door.

He walked back quickly, but stopped at the turn to check the floor. The surface beyond was intact.

Martin was waiting outside.

---

"We're moving the people out either way," Inessa said after hearing him out. "That's our job, not yours. Don't stand here just because you think everyone is waiting for your decision."

Asgar looked at the drive.

A service car stood far beyond the new barrier. He could walk to it, get in, and return to his room. The bread would still be on the table. The specialist would connect the phone. In the evening, a message might come saying the booth had disappeared on its own.

Or another kind of message.

"How far can you move people away?" he asked.

"As far as needed. But if the boundary starts changing faster than we can evacuate people, it'll be bad."

She spoke evenly, neither persuading nor hurrying him.

Martin stood slightly aside.

"Can you do anything else?" Asgar asked him.

He shook his head.

"There's nothing else to turn off without causing an accident ourselves. And we don't know how to close this boundary."

Asgar shifted his gaze to the screen.

The crack in the floor had grown longer. The employee beside it compared two frames, switching back and forth. In one, it ended at the seam. In the other, it crossed it. On the nearby plan, another area flashed on the other side of the booth; Inessa asked to keep both. The ends of the changes lay at almost the same distance from the red body even though the cameras still disagreed about exactly where the floor ran.

Asgar watched until the switching stopped.

He thought about Katya.

Last time before entering, she had been somewhere close—behind glass, in the city, in the same time. Now more than a century and a half lay between them. There was nowhere to call.

The decision was still his.

"I'm going in."

Martin lowered his head, then looked at him again.

"Do you really think it'll help?"

"After my previous entries, the changes stopped. Maybe that's coincidence. But I don't know what else to do."

"Has she been wrong before?"

Asgar remembered four days. Twenty-five years. Katya behind the glass.

"She never promised to send me home. I kept hearing a little more than she was saying."

He took the work tablet from his pocket.

The message about the scheduled inspection was still on the screen.

Asgar handed it to Martin.

"Return this, please. It's issued in my name."

Martin took the device but kept looking at Asgar.

"Your room stays. I won't remove anything from it."

Asgar nodded.

"And the recordings. I marked one, but didn't finish it."

"We'll deal with it."

He said it in the local language. Asgar understood before the translation.

They both noticed and smiled for a second.

Then Martin held out his hand.

Asgar shook it. His palm was dry and warm. Asgar wanted to say something ordinary so he would not leave the man with that expression.

"Water the plant less."

Martin let out a short breath through his nose.

"Come back and check it yourself."

Asgar did not promise.

---

This time, he walked without the tablet.

Inessa stood by the side entrance beside Martin. Before Asgar turned the corner, she raised a hand to show the route was still clear. He returned the gesture.

After that there was only the ringing.

Asgar stopped by the booth. The crack ran to the left of his route, through the middle of the main passage. A strip of intact flooring still remained by the wall.

He opened the door and lifted the receiver.

"I'm going to enter. The floor on this side is still flat."

The woman asked him to wait.

Asgar stood before the threshold holding the door. In that time he managed to remember the mug on the table, clean clothes on the shelf, the road to the sea. He had not chosen this city. But now he knew how to walk through it in the evening.

"You can enter," the woman said. "Once you're inside, close the door completely."

He stepped over the threshold.

The cord brushed his wrist. Asgar shifted the receiver, freed his hand, and pulled the door toward him. Just before it closed, he looked into the passage once more.

Empty. Light on the floor. Far beyond the turn were people who knew him by name.

The door settled into the frame.

Asgar braced his shoulder against the wall, waiting for the jolt.

"Closed."

The woman began to answer, but her voice cut off.

The floor swayed. Asgar bent his knees and held himself steady with his free palm against the frame. For several moments the lit passage remained before his eyes, then the lights stretched into bands and went out.

He remained standing in the same position.

Beyond the glass was darkness.

Then his eyes began to make out a pale sky above the black edge of a ruined wall. Wind drove dust along the ground. Where a flat surface had been a second ago, debris protruded.

Asgar raised the receiver to his ear.

"Are you there?"

Only a faint hiss remained in the receiver.

He slowly took his hand off the door handle.
