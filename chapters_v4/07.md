Chapter 7. Familiar Eyes

The coordinator pulled the screen closer to herself.

Asgar watched her face. While she read, her lips moved slightly, and he tried to guess the answer before she said it. The woman opened another record and checked something against the entered data.

"There is an active contact. We'll try to reach her now."

"So she's alive?"

"Yes. The contact was updated recently. We'll call now."

She put on a headset and turned to the screen. Asgar straightened and took his hands off the table. His crumpled napkin lay on the chair beside him; he picked it up and put it in his pocket.

The coordinator waited for an answer.

Then she said:

"Ekaterina? Good afternoon. I'm calling from the service center regarding a case connected to Asgar."

Asgar leaned forward.

He could not hear Katya. He only saw the woman at the desk stop typing and place one hand beside the keyboard.

"No, this isn't a request for your documents. Today a man came to us with his identification card. His identity has been confirmed against archived biometric data."

She listened to the reply.

"Yes. He's here."

Asgar stood up.

The coordinator looked at him and raised a palm: wait.

"Yes, I understand. Let's turn on video. You'll see him yourself."

Asgar stood with his thigh against the table. His legs got in each other's way; he shifted one and hit the chair.

The woman removed the headset.

"She agreed."

He sat down before she could ask him to. Ran a hand through his hair and lowered it to his knee. The screen was turned toward him. For several seconds it showed only an empty field.

Then Katya appeared.

Asgar recognized her before he had time to take her in.

"Katya."

She was sitting very close to the camera. The edge of her hand was visible at the bottom of the image; the picture wobbled as if she had moved the device and had not immediately found somewhere to rest it.

Katya looked at him.

He waited for her to speak and could not continue himself. Her hair was shorter. A pale strand lay by her face, and she kept brushing it back. He remembered that movement with long hair: fingers going behind the ear, then sliding a little farther along the neck. Now her hand stopped sooner.

"Say something else," she asked.

Her voice was lower than it had been that morning. But the last word broke in the familiar way.

"I came out. I started calling right away, but my phone here doesn't..."

Katya covered her mouth with her hand.

Asgar fell silent.

She looked over her fingers without taking her eyes off him. He saw the lines beside the bridge of her nose, the pale skin beneath her eyes. For a second he wanted to move the image closer, sharpen it, remove anything that might be the fault of a bad camera.

"Where were you?" she asked.

"In the booth."

"All this time?"

"For me... Katya, I spoke to you today. About the folding cot. You told me to lower the brace."

Her hand slowly dropped.

"The brace?"

"The leg kept folding. I wedged a tile under it. You said there should be a brace on the side."

Katya looked away. He saw part of the room behind her shoulder: a pale wall, a narrow shelf, a cup on a table. Nothing familiar.

"I don't remember," she said.

He wanted to remind her. Had already opened his mouth.

Katya looked at him again.

"I remember you called. I remember the last conversation. But the cot—I don't."

Asgar lowered his eyes.

His ID lay on the table between them. That morning he had checked that he had it before walking across the yard toward the red door.

The coordinator quietly stood up.

"I'll be outside. If you need help, call."

She went out and pulled the door almost closed.

Asgar waited for the click.

"Are you alone?"

"Right now, yes."

"The children?"

"They're both fine."

He exhaled so sharply that it hurt beneath his ribs.

Katya leaned closer.

"They're both alive, Asgar. Everything is fine. I'll call them."

"Are they far?"

"The older one is outside the city. The younger is here in Baku. But first I..."

She reached for something outside the frame and brought her hand back empty.

"First I need to understand for myself that it's really you."

"It is me."

"I can see that."

Katya wiped her cheek with the heel of her palm. Asgar ran a hand over his own face too, though it was dry.

"I can come to you," he said. "Give me the address. They've already checked me here, I can..."

"I'll come myself. Stay there."

She stood up. Part of her shoulder remained in frame, then the room without her. Asgar heard a drawer being pulled open.

"Katya?"

"I'm here. Looking for my bag."

"Don't disconnect."

She came back and sat down.

"I'm not."

There was a thin chain around her neck. Asgar looked at it because looking at her face all the time was becoming difficult. He recognized Katya in the movement of her lips, in the way she lifted her chin slightly while waiting for an answer. Then his eyes caught on something unfamiliar, and he had to begin recognizing her all over again.

"Are you at home?" he asked.

"Yes."

"We don't live there anymore?"

Katya shook her head.

"I moved."

"When?"

"A long time ago. When the younger one started living on his own."

Asgar glanced at the closed door. Suddenly he needed somewhere else to look.

The younger boy had been telling him about a toy car today. Breathing into the microphone, upset that Dad didn't know when he'd be back.

"He lives by himself?"

"By himself. At first I checked every morning to see whether he'd woken up. Then he asked me to stop."

Katya tried to smile.

"You would have taken his side."

Asgar nodded, though he had no idea whose side he would have taken. He had not had a single conversation with that grown man.

"Send me a picture."

"I will. Better yet, talk to them yourselves. But I'll tell them first. I don't want someone else calling them."

She picked up a second phone or something like one. Asgar could see only its edge in her hand.

"Wait a little longer," he asked.

Katya put it back down.

For a while they sat in silence.

"I saw in the documents," Asgar said. "That I was..."

He could not say the word.

"Yes."

"You knew?"

She slowly straightened.

"I filed the application."

He looked at her.

Katya rubbed the edge of the table with her thumb.

"There were things that had to be closed out, and no one would let me close them without you. They kept saying: bring the ruling. I'd go somewhere else, and they asked for the same thing. I constantly had to prove why you couldn't come yourself."

"I'm not asking why you..."

"You are."

Asgar looked away.

"Probably."

"I didn't want to. Then I had to anyway."

He imagined her in a courthouse corridor with a folder. Tried to place himself beside her, to take the papers, speak for her, and it only made it worse.

"Sorry."

Katya pressed her lips together.

"I can't talk about that right now."

"Okay."

"And don't think I spent all that time just sitting and waiting for the phone to ring. I worked. The children grew up. We argued, went places, renovated. Once a pipe burst at night and I was scooping water off the stairs. A lot happened."

She stopped and looked somewhere below the camera.

"I just don't know where to start telling you."

Asgar reached toward the screen. His fingers touched the cold edge.

"Wherever you want."

Katya noticed the movement. Her face crumpled; she closed her eyes and shook her head.

"I need to see you properly."

"Come."

"I'm leaving now."

She reached for her bag again, this time leaving it on her lap. Keys clinked inside.

Asgar suddenly remembered Katya's voice in the stadium service corridor: "Then don't drag it out. And don't forget the water, please." She had been smiling then—he could hear it in her voice. He had planned to buy water and walk into the kitchen where Katya was fussing with the cake.

"And the cake?" he asked.

Katya froze.

"What cake?"

"That day. The sixteenth. Did you..."

He stopped when he heard his own question.

Katya looked at him for a long time.

"We cut it that evening."

Asgar took his hand away from the screen.

He lowered his head. Pale dust from the repair depot marked the jeans near his knee. Asgar rubbed it with his fingers, making the stain wider.

"What are you wearing?" Katya asked.

He did not understand at first.

"A T-shirt. From the bag at the depot."

"Stand up."

Asgar pushed the chair back and stood in front of the screen.

Katya looked up at him from below. Asgar was about to sit again.

"Wait," she said.

He stayed standing.

"I folded it," Katya said. "That one."

Asgar took hold of the hem of the T-shirt.

"Yes."

She turned away and began searching for something in her bag. She rummaged through it for a long time, though the keys were already lying in front of her.

---

When the coordinator returned, Katya asked for the exact address. The woman explained where the center was, then looked at Asgar.

"One more thing. You said the booth appears near you and has already damaged equipment. There are a lot of people here."

Asgar stood up.

He had managed to forget.

"I should go outside."

"Yes. They'll take you into the yard. I've already called the duty service; they're looking for a separate room."

Katya listened from the screen.

"Can it appear again?" she asked.

Asgar turned toward her.

"I don't know. The woman on the phone said the connection could remain after I came out."

"Did you speak to her here?"

"No. Only before I went in."

Katya looked at the coordinator.

"Tell me where you're taking him. I'll come there."

"Keep the connection open. As soon as we have an address, I'll send it."

Asgar picked up his ID and mobile from the table.

"Katya, I..."

"I'm connected. They have my number now."

He nodded.

She looked at him for several more seconds.

"Just make sure they tell me where first. Don't leave until I know."

---

The yard smelled of heated garbage bins. Asgar stood on an open patch between the rear entrance and the fence. A center employee remained by the door and asked people coming out to use the other side.

They had to wait for the car.

Two police officers came into the yard. The coordinator met them by the door and showed them the case number. One stayed with her; the other came over to Asgar, introduced himself, and explained that the duty unit had received a report of a person found from a closed missing-person case. The same old photograph was on his screen. He asked Asgar's name, matched it to the record, and lingered on his face.

"You came to the center yourself?"

"Yes. I need to get to my wife. She's already on her way."

The officer returned to the coordinator without closing the image. Asgar heard a question about repeating the verification. The woman opened the duty specialist's conclusion; the officer read it and called his unit. This time, when he named the year of the disappearance, he turned his whole body toward Asgar.

A woman with a phone stopped beyond the fence. Asgar turned away, but after a few steps found himself facing her again. The officer walked to the fence, asked her not to film, and stayed there until she left.

During that time Asgar took his phone out twice to look at the photo of the children, but the screen no longer came on. He pressed the button again, held it longer, then put the mobile away. The charger was still in the trailer, twenty-five years away.

The coordinator came out carrying a tablet and a small bag. She handed him water.

"We have a place. A technical building attached to the archive unit. There's an empty hall with a separate entrance. A duty team is already on the way."

"Archive?"

"Your case is stored there. They requested the materials from today's report as soon as the identity confirmation came through."

"Are the police going there too?"

"No. They've passed the information to the unit. There's nothing else for them to do here."

"Do they know about the booth?"

"The case contains recordings and reports. I don't handle the technical side."

Asgar unscrewed the bottle cap.

"Does Katya know the address?"

"Yes. She got it."

Asgar screwed the cap back on and walked toward the car.

---

A wide ramp led down into the technical hall. Asgar followed a building employee down it; the coordinator stayed above, finishing a call.

It was cooler below. Pale stripes on the floor marked the path to the far door. Empty metal carts stood along the wall, a coiled cable lay dark in one corner. The employee moved the nearest cart into the next room and asked Asgar to stay by the entrance for now.

"Has this been empty long?" Asgar asked.

"Since spring. The equipment was moved."

The man checked a side door and came back.

"No outsiders come in here. If you notice anything, say so immediately."

Asgar looked at the ceiling. Concrete beams, pipes, lights. After the street, the room seemed too quiet.

He told the employee about the shortened tripod leg. The man listened, glanced at the remaining carts, and called someone on the radio. A few minutes later those were removed too.

The coordinator came down carrying a portable screen. Behind her, a table and two chairs were brought in. They were placed by the entrance, leaving the middle of the hall clear.

"While we wait for the specialist, you can look at part of the archive," she said. "You asked about the man at the stadium. They found the recording Kerimov referred to."

Asgar sat down.

The familiar corridor appeared on the screen.

He immediately recognized the place where he had stood with his mobile. The image was brighter than the one Rauf had shown him. A red wall was visible in the distance. Asgar leaned closer, placing his hands on either side of the screen.

The booth door opened.

A man climbed out and paused beside the body. Started down the corridor.

Asgar did not look at himself, though now he could see his own figure too. He watched the man: lowered shoulders, careful step, brief stop.

"Slower."

The coordinator reduced the speed.

The man turned his head.

Asgar raised a hand.

"Here. Stop."

The face remained a small pale blot.

"Can you zoom in?"

She enlarged the area. The space around the head broke into blurred rectangles. For a second the coordinator returned to the original scale. The distortion did not fill the whole corridor: nearer the turn, the tile joints became straight again, and the interference ended so abruptly it was as though it had an edge of its own.

Asgar stared until his eyes began to ache.

"In twenty-five years you couldn't do anything with this?"

The coordinator opened the list of files attached to the recording.

"There are processed versions. I'll show you the latest."

She showed him a second file. The contours were clearer, but the face still did not resolve into someone who could be recognized.

"Anything else?"

"That's all they managed."

Asgar pushed the screen away from himself.

He did not know what he had expected. That the future would place a name beneath the blurred head. That the man would have been found, questioned, would have told them everything long ago, and all Asgar would need to do was read.

"Did they even look for him?"

The coordinator opened several documents.

"They did. They checked exits, neighboring cameras, witnesses. His identity was never established."

Asgar looked at the document list from the side.

"And Kerimov?"

The coordinator found the relevant field.

"He handled the case until 2019. Then he handed it over. But he returned to it twice more, unofficially. The last note is seven years after your disappearance."

Seven years after that, Asgar himself had already been legally dead for a year. He lowered his eyes to the edge of the table. The man who had told him that's exactly why I haven't closed the case had kept it open after everyone else closed it.

In the paused frame, the man was still looking toward the booth.

Asgar remembered stepping aside to let him pass. He could have gone over, made sure he did not need help. Could have delayed him for a few seconds.

He reached for the play button and watched the clip again.

The man disappeared around the corner.

---

A building employee brought over the investigation reports. He placed them on the table in electronic form and remained standing beside it while the coordinator opened the relevant section.

When she reached the medical record, the man asked Asgar to turn toward the light. Asgar did, expecting another photograph. The employee did not take anything. He looked at the date on the report and moved his hand away from the screen.

"I prepared these materials for transfer," he said. "In the spring. There were only records there."

Asgar pulled his chair closer to the table. He needed to read what they had found over all those years.

The first item on the list was the medical report from August twentieth. Asgar opened it and read one line twice: no signs of a condition consistent with four days without water, food, or sleep were found.

Asgar flipped through several pages. The dates changed. The same frames appeared among diagrams and tables. He stopped on words he understood, trying to connect them into an answer.

"I need to know one thing," he said. "Is there another device like this here? Anything that can send me back?"

The employee shook his head.

"We don't have a working device."

"You don't. What about anywhere else?"

"I don't know. But not in these materials. They weren't able to reproduce the transition."

Asgar went back to the beginning of the document.

"But you studied it."

"They studied the recordings and what remained after the appearances. The cabin itself was gone after your disappearance."

The man pointed to one of the sections.

"These are the attempts to reproduce the effect. They couldn't send a person or an object to another time."

Asgar read the line he was pointing to.

"And that's it?"

The employee did not answer immediately.

"For you, a few hours passed. This work took years."

Asgar looked up at him, then back at the screen.

Below were names. Dates of interim reports. Signatures beneath failed experiments.

"Sorry," he said. "I didn't mean..."

He did not finish and closed the document.

The coordinator checked a message.

"Ekaterina is arriving. An employee will meet her upstairs."

Asgar turned toward the ramp.

For the first time since stepping out of the booth, he knew exactly where Katya would be coming from.

He stood up, walked a few steps, and stopped by the stripe on the floor. He wanted to meet her at the entrance, but looked at the empty center of the hall and stayed where he was.

"Tell her I'm here."

"She knows."

Asgar put a hand into his pocket and felt the dead phone. Took it out and ran a finger across the dark glass.

The light above the table reflected in the screen.

He tilted the phone.

The reflection split in two.

Asgar froze, then looked up.

The light hung where it had been. But near the far wall, a thin yellow floor marking split in the same way: one line ran toward the door, another lay alongside it for a short stretch.

He blinked.

The lines merged.

"Look over there," Asgar said.

The employee turned.

For an instant, a red edge appeared on the concrete wall.

Asgar stepped back from the middle of the hall.

"Don't bring Katya down yet."
